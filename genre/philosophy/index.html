Best Long Philosophy Audiobooks

Explore our selection of epic Philosophy audiobooks.

Welcome to our curated collection of the best and longest Philosophy audiobooks, all 20+ hours in length.

Epic Audiobooks in this Genre

  • Atlas Shrugged by Ayn Rand (63h)

    Narrated by: Scott Brick

    63 hours of philosophy and railways. Love it or hate it, this massive treatise demands your full attention.

  • Dune by Frank Herbert (21h 2m)

    Narrated by: Scott Brick, Orlagh Cassidy, Euan Morton, Simon Vance

    21 hours on Arrakis. The sci-fi masterpiece that defined the genre, with a unique full-cast production.

  • War and Peace by Leo Tolstoy (61h 8m)

    Narrated by: Thandiwe Newton

    The mountaintop of literature. 61 hours of life, love, and history. Intimidating, but incredibly human.

  • Shantaram by Gregory David Roberts (43h)

    Narrated by: Humphrey Bower

    43 hours in Bombay's underworld. A vivid, gritty memoir-novel that feels like a lived life.

  • Crime and Punishment by Fyodor Dostoevsky (26h 11m)

    Narrated by: Anthony Heald

    26 hours inside a killer's mind. A psychological thriller that predates the genre by a century.

  • The Brothers Karamazov by Fyodor Dostoevsky (37h 5m)

    Narrated by: Luke Thompson

    A 37-hour murder mystery with a soul. An intense family drama and philosophical debate.

  • The Fall of Hyperion by Dan Simmons (19h 54m)

    Narrated by: Victor Bevine

    20 hours of war. The stunning conclusion to the Hyperion cantos delivers on every promise.

  • Anathem by Neal Stephenson (32h 45m)

    Narrated by: William Dufris, Oliver Wyman, Tavia Gilbert, Neal Stephenson

    33 hours of ideas. Monks, math, and multiverse theory clash in Stephenson's most intellectual epic.

  • Stranger in a Strange Land by Robert A. Heinlein (16h 3m)

    Narrated by: Christopher Hurt

    16 hours challenging culture. Heinlein's counter-culture classic about a human raised on Mars.

  • Dune Messiah by Frank Herbert (9h 7m)

    Narrated by: Scott Brick, Simon Vance, et al.

    9 hours of consequences. A tighter, tragic coda to Dune that deconstructs the hero myth.

  • The Magic Mountain by Thomas Mann (35h 15m)

    Narrated by: David Rintoul

    35 hours on the mountain. A philosophical novel of ideas set in a pre-war sanitarium.

  • East of Eden by John Steinbeck (25h 32m)

    Narrated by: Simon Manyonda

    Steinbeck's 25-hour magnum opus. A biblical allegory of good and evil set in the Salinas Valley.

  • 2666 by Roberto Bolaño (39h 15m)

    Narrated by: John Lee, Armando Dur\u00e1n, G. Valmont Thomas, Scott Brick, Grover Gardner

    39 hours of hypnotic literary mystery. Five interlocking novels that orbit the dark heart of the 20th century.

  • Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks by Yuval Noah Harari (17h 52m)

    Narrated by: Vidish Athavale

    Essential 18-hour reading for the AI era. Harari traces information networks from the Stone Age to our uncertain future.

  • The Fyodor Dostoyevsky Complete Collection by Fyodor Dostoyevsky (266h 4m)

    Narrated by: David Rintoul, Jonathan Keeble, Malk Williams, Emma Gregory, Roger May, Ben Allen

    266 hours. The ultimate Russian collection. Longer than walking from London to Rome.

  • The Player of Games by Iain M. Banks (11h 26m)

    Narrated by: Peter Kenny

    11 hours of philosophical space opera. A master strategist is dispatched to unseat a brutal empire via an epic game.

  • The Human Factor by Graham Greene (9h 36m)

    Narrated by: Tim Pigott-Smith

    Over 9 hours of quiet literary suspense. An aging MI6 officer leaks secrets for love rather than communist ideology.

  • The Man Who Laughs by Victor Hugo (22h 28m)

    Narrated by: Simon Vance

    A mutilated boy with a permanent carved smile. 22.5 hours of Gothic tragedy and aristocratic satire narrated by Simon Vance.
