Best Long Cozy Crime & Village Mystery Audiobooks

The Best Long Cozy Crime & Village Mystery Audiobooks for Armchair Detectives.

Tea, Gossip, and Foul Play

Step away from the blood-slicked crime scenes of Nordic Noir and settle into the deceptively quiet world of village greens, eccentric pensioners, and locked-room puzzles. These 10+ hour audiobooks prove that a cunning amateur sleuth, a sharp wit, and a fresh pot of tea are all you need to catch a killer.

Books in this Collection

  • The Thursday Murder Club by Richard Osman (12h 25m)

    The Pensioners Who Catch Killers

    Does Lesley Manville’s razor-sharp, aristocratic delivery elevate these eighty-year-old sleuths from cozy caricatures to genuinely brilliant detectives? Read our full review and Soundcheck to see our verdict.

  • Magpie Murders by Anthony Horowitz (15h 15m)

    A Murder Wrapped Inside a Manuscript

    Can dual narrators successfully juggle a golden-age 1950s detective story and a modern-day publishing murder without losing the listener's focus? Read our full review and Soundcheck to see if this meta-mystery earns your credit.

  • The Appeal by Janice Hallett (12h 31m)

    Murder in the Local Theatre Troupe

    Can a full-cast audio production turn a pile of passive-aggressive community theatre emails into a pulse-pounding, 12-hour whodunnit? Read our full review and Soundcheck to see how this format thrives on audio.

  • Still Life by Louise Penny (10h 38m)

    Murder in the Woods of Three Pines

    Can an archery murder in a forgotten Canadian hamlet launch one of the most comforting, intellectually rigorous mystery sagas in audio? Read our full review and Soundcheck to see our verdict on Gamache’s debut.

  • The Maid by Nita Prose (10h 1m)

    The Invisible Hotel Detective

    Lauren Ambrose voices a highly eccentric, neurodivergent protagonist, but does her delivery maintain empathy across ten hours without leaning into caricature? Read our full review and Soundcheck to see if this locked-room mystery shines.

  • The Sweetness at the Bottom of the Pie by Alan Bradley (6h 36m)

    An 11-Year-Old Chemistry Genius Catches a Killer

    Does Jayne Entwistle capture the precocious, delightfully macabre wit of Flavia de Luce, or does a child narrator grate across six and a half hours? Read our full review and Soundcheck to see our verdict on this charming debut.
