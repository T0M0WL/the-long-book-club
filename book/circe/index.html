About this Audiobook

The Sorceress Who Forged Her Own Divinity on the Shores of Aiaia

Madeline Miller accomplishes something genuinely miraculous in *Circe*: she takes a mythological archetype traditionally reduced to a seductive, villainous obstacle in Homer’s *Odyssey* and reimagines her as an expansive, deeply introspective protagonist. Born into the blazing, opulent palace of the sun titan Helios where petty vanity and casual cruelty reign supreme, Circe is dismissed from birth as dull-voiced, plain, and hopelessly powerless. Yet her unyielding capacity for empathy drives her toward forbidden pharmacopeia, unlocking an ancient witchcraft drawn from the blood of primordial gods that terrifies even Zeus into decreeing her eternal banishment to the solitary island of Aiaia. Here, amid taming island wolves, tending venomous herbs, and weaving enchanted tapestries, she transforms bitter isolation into sovereign autonomy.

What makes this narrative so profoundly compelling across its twelve-hour performance is how Miller relentlessly deconstructs divine arrogance against the tender fragility of mortal existence. Gods throughout this tale are portrayed not as enlightened cosmic guardians, but as capricious, predatory monarchs whose immortality breeds profound emotional numbness and moral rot. Circe’s interactions with towering figures of antiquity—from the labyrinthine agony of the Minotaur’s grotesque birth alongside her defiant sister Pasiphaë to the terrifying hunger of Scylla and the weary, bronze-clad swagger of Odysseus—force her to reckon with the heavy human collateral of divine vanity. Her island refuge becomes a theater where she challenges the Olympians on her own terms, turning entitled, predatory men into swine not out of wanton cruelty, but as an act of absolute self-preservation in an unforgiving ancient world.

Narrator Soundcheck: Perdita Weeks

Perdita Weeks delivers a career-defining performance that elevated Circe into an undisputed modern audiobook classic. Her voice possesses a soft, luminous dignity laced with steely undercurrents, perfectly mirroring Circe’s evolution from an insecure, mocked girl into an indomitable sorceress. Weeks handles ancient Greek cadences, mythological incantations, and intimate domestic confessions with aristocratic composure and genuine emotional vulnerability. Her pacing never rushes, giving every herbal draught and divine threat space to breathe and mesmerise across twelve spellbinding hours.
