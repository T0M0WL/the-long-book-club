The Little Friend

Author: Donna Tartt | Narrator: Donna Tartt

About this Audiobook

In a stifling Mississippi town, a precocious twelve-year-old girl dedicates her summer to avenging her brother's unsolved hanging twelve years earlier.

Donna Tartt's Haunting, Author-Narrated Southern Gothic Masterwork

Spanning twenty-four and a half atmospheric hours, *The Little Friend* is the long-awaited second novel from Pulitzer Prize-winner Donna Tartt, arriving between her seminal debut The Secret History and her Dickensian masterpiece The Goldfinch. Set in Alexandria, Mississippi in the 1970s, the novel revolves around twelve-year-old Harriet Cleve Dufresnes, a solitary, fiercely bookish girl growing up in a decaying family scarred by tragedy. Twelve years earlier, Harriet’s nine-year-old brother Robin was found hanged from a tupelo tree in their yard—a crime that remained entirely unsolved and hollowed out her mother, father, and eccentric matriarchal aunts.

Convinced that Robin’s killer was his impoverished childhood classmate Danny Ratliff, Harriet embarks on a relentless, clandestine campaign of vengeance during a sweltering summer vacation, aided by her loyal best friend Hely. Rather than a conventional whodunit, Tartt delivers a deeply layered Southern Gothic tragedy steeped in class resentment, religious fanatical cults, venomous water moccasins, and methamphetamine labs in the Mississippi woods. The audio edition is elevated immeasurably by Donna Tartt’s own performance: her authentic, lilting Mississippi cadence imbues the humid landscape with cinematic sensory richness, making every cicada buzz and old porch conversation pulse with latent dread.

For listeners who prize prose craftsmanship, rich character nuance, and exceptional audiobook immersion for a single credit, *The Little Friend* is a treasure. Tartt’s voice adds an intimate, storytelling gravitas that print cannot replicate, completing her triad of monumental novels alongside The Secret History and The Goldfinch. It stands as a brilliant contribution to Literary Fiction that will transport listeners into a lost Southern childhood of extraordinary depth and haunting beauty.

Narrator Soundcheck: Donna Tartt

Donna Tartt proves herself to be an extraordinarily gifted narrator, bringing her native Mississippi heritage directly into Harriet’s world. Her smoky, drawling cadence gives the novel an authentic, porch-side intimacy, capturing the languid rhythm of small-town gossip, the fierce bravado of twelve-year-old Harriet, and the terrifying volatility of the criminal Ratliff family. Tartt’s voice never wavers in emotional truth over twenty-four hours, lending the audiobook a hypnotic, spellbinding resonance that print simply cannot convey.
