About this Audiobook

Croaker, physician and annalist of the legendary mercenary brotherhood known as the Black Company, records their bloody service under the dark sorceress known as the Lady in an unforgiving war of conquest.

Mud, Blood, and Mercenary Cynicism: The Legendary Patriarch of Grimdark

Published in 1984, Glen Cook’s *The Black Company* is widely acknowledged by authors from Steven Erikson to Joe Abercrombie as the foundational text that gave birth to modern military grimdark. Rejecting the romanticized, high-chivalric conventions of traditional epic fantasy, Cook—a Vietnam War veteran—delivered an authentic grunt’s-eye chronicle of warfare. Narrated by Croaker, the world-weary physician and annalist of the mercenary brotherhood, the novel chronicles the company’s pragmatic decision to sign on with Soulcatcher, an undead sorcerer-general serving the immortal, terrifying sorceress known as the Lady.

Listening to *The Black Company* on audio is an exceptionally intimate and gripping experience, thanks to Marc Vietor’s dry, gravelly, and unhurried performance. Vietor inhabits Croaker’s voice with the quiet weariness of a field medic who has patched up too many butchered comrades and witnessed too many atrocities to believe in righteous crusades. The chemistry among the soldiers—particularly the petty, centuries-old magical prank feud between pint-sized wizards Goblin and One-Eye, alongside the stoic discipline of Captain and Lieutenant—provides brilliant, authentic comedic respite between terrifying, blood-soaked skirmishes. Fans who appreciate the raw squad loyalty in Gardens of the Moon or the unflinching brutality of The Blade Itself will find this landmark classic completely irresistible.

At just over 10 hours, *The Black Company* represents lean, filler-free military storytelling that delivers maximum atmospheric punch per Audible credit. By stripping away epic fantasy grandiosity, Cook created a boots-on-the-ground masterpiece of soldier camaraderie and survival. Cook wastes no time on unnecessary floral descriptions, focusing instead on tactical maneuver, moral compromise, and the brotherhood forged under enemy fire. Cook's genius is his refusal to glamorize combat; the Black Company complains about bad food, wet boots, and lousy pay while holding the line against nightmares. It is an indispensable, historically vital listening milestone that belongs in the permanent library of every true fantasy connoisseur seeking the raw origins of the genre.

Narrator Soundcheck: Marc Vietor

Marc Vietor provides the definitive audio voice for Croaker, narrating with a gruff, dry, and laconic delivery that perfectly matches the tone of a war journal written by campfire light. He balances Croaker’s cynical outward detachment with subtle flashes of compassion and romantic longing, while providing delightfully eccentric, scratchy voices for squabbling mages Goblin and One-Eye. His steady pacing and military discipline keep the tactical battlefield maneuvers crisp, tense, and utterly believable throughout.
