About this Audiobook

The explosive climax to The First Law trilogy as the Union tears itself apart under invasion, peasant revolt, and royal succession.

The Pinnacle of Modern Grimdark: A 27-Hour Tour de Force of Betrayal and Subversion

*Last Argument of Kings* is the devastating, exhilarating payoff that cements Joe Abercrombie as the peerless architect of dark fantasy. The King of the Union lies dying, peasant revolts burn in the countryside, and northern barbarians hammer against the borders while the Gurkish armada descends upon the capital itself. As the ancient war between magi Bayaz and Khalul finally spills into open slaughter, Sand dan Glokta faces the most dangerous political labyrinth of his life, Logen Ninefingers returns North to settle a lifetime of bloody debts, and Jezal dan Luthar finds his romantic daydreams shattered by the ruthless machinery of power.

Across twenty-seven glorious hours, Steven Pacey turns this sprawling finale into an emotional and dramatic rollercoaster. What makes this conclusion so legendary is Abercrombie's refusal to offer comforting genre tropes; every victory is pyrrhic, every noble ideal is weaponized, and the true monstrous puppet masters are finally unmasked. Pacey's delivery of Logen's transformation into the Bloody-Nine is genuinely terrifying on audio, shaking the listener with animalistic fury before dropping back into weary, tragic heartbreak. Fans of epic multi-perspective climaxes like The Blade Itself and Words of Radiance will find this auditory experience incomparable.

For the price of a single Audible credit, twenty-seven hours of flawless narration and merciless plotting represent the highest echelon of audiobook value. It ties together hundreds of narrative threads with shocking precision, delivering an unforgettable conclusion that lingers in the mind for months.

Narrator Soundcheck: Steven Pacey

Steven Pacey's performance in *Last Argument of Kings* is the crowning achievement of a legendary audio trilogy. His modulation between intimate psychological monologues and the roaring chaos of the Battle of Adua is masterly. Even after twenty-seven continuous hours, Pacey's vocal energy never flags, providing distinct accents, idiosyncratic mannerisms, and razor-sharp emotional subtext for over forty individual characters without a single misstep.
