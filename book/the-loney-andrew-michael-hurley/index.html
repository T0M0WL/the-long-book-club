About this Audiobook

On the bleak, tide-swept Lancashire coast in the 1970s, two brothers accompany their devout Catholic mother on an Easter pilgrimage to Coldbarrow—where ancient pagan superstition and the shifting sea refuse to stay buried.

Eleven Hours of Bleak Lancashire Tides and Haunting British Folk Horror in Hurley's Award Winner

Before coastal roads carved up the northwest English shore, 'The Loney' was a desolate stretch of Lancashire mudflats where the tide swept in fast enough to drown the careless. In the grim, rain-soaked Easter of 1976, a teenage narrator known only as Tonto travels with his deeply devout Catholic mother, his mute older brother Hanny, and a nervous parish priest to an isolated stone house called Moorings—hoping for a religious miracle. Instead, the family collides with strange local villagers, pagan superstition, and secrets buried in the shifting tidal caves.

In audio format, Andrew Michael Hurley's Costa First Novel Award winner stands as the quiet, bone-chilling cornerstone of modern British Folk Horror and Literary Fiction. Rather than relying on monsters jumping from shadows, Hurley builds suffocating dread through wet wool, incense smoke, seagulls crying over black water, and unspoken family guilt—standing proudly alongside Blackwater: The Complete Saga on our Folk Horror shelf.

Spanning eleven hours and sixteen minutes for a single Audible credit, *The Loney* is an unforgettable atmospheric triumph for dark winter walks.

Narrator Soundcheck: Richard Burnip

British narrator Richard Burnip delivers an understated, deeply haunting performance across more than eleven hours. Burnip's quiet northern cadence mirrors Tonto's guarded retrospective memory perfectly, letting long silences, bleak coastal winds, and sharp moments of religious fanaticism resonate with chilling authenticity.
