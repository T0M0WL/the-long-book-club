About this Audiobook

In a sun-drenched, bicycle-powered 2065 Orange County built on solar architecture and municipal water commons, young green-party architect Kevin Claiborne fights a civic battle to save the town's last wild undeveloped hill.

Over Eleven Sunlit Hours Inside the Foundational Blueprint of Modern Solarpunk

Long before the word 'solarpunk' existed, Kim Stanley Robinson wrote *Pacific Edge* (1990)—the third and culminating standalone volume of his *Three Californias Triptych*, where each book imagines a completely different possible future for Orange County rather than a sequential plot. While its companion volumes explore nuclear collapse and corporate cyberpunk, *Pacific Edge* dares to imagine a plausible, hard-won ecological utopia in 2065: a sunlit, bicycle-powered town called El Modena where cars are banned, houses are retrofitted with passive solar architecture, and local democracy governs water rights and land commons.

Rather than presenting a boring, conflict-free paradise, Robinson centers the story on Kevin Claiborne, an idealistic young green-party architect and town-council newcomer caught in a fierce municipal battle to stop a charismatic mayor from bulldozing the town's last wild undeveloped hill—interwoven with raw journal entries from his grandfather recalling the messy, painful legal struggles that built this greener world in the first place. On audio, these intimate council meetings, softball games, love triangles, and coastal hikes feel wonderfully tactile and alive, forming a direct bridge to Robinson’s later Solarpunk milestones New York 2140 and The Ministry for the Future.

At eleven hours and twenty-seven minutes for a single Audible credit—and requiring zero prior reading of the other *Three Californias* standalones—*Pacific Edge* is the essential, deeply human gateway into hopeful ecological science fiction.

Narrator Soundcheck: Stefan Rudnicki

Grammy-winning narrator Stefan Rudnicki brings his signature warm, deep-register baritone and thoughtful conversational ease to eleven and a half sunlit hours in 2065 California. Rudnicki excels at capturing both Kevin's earnest civic idealism and the gruff, weathered wisdom of his grandfather's historical journal entries—turning local town-hall debates, quiet hikes, and emotional dilemmas into an unusually comforting, grounded listen.
