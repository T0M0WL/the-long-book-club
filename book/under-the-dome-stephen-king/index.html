About this Audiobook

On an entirely normal, beautiful fall day in Chester's Mill, Maine, the town is inexplicably and suddenly sealed off from the rest of the world by an invisible force field.

A Relentless Thirty-Four-Hour Pressure Cooker of Small-Town Societal Collapse in Audio

On an otherwise peaceful autumn morning in western Maine, an invisible, impenetrable, and semi-permeable dome violently slams down over the town of Chester's Mill, severing its residents from the outside world in an instant. As trapped families witness planes crashing into the clear barrier and severed limbs falling at the perimeter, local democracy collapses with terrifying speed. Second Selectman Big Jim Rennie—a corrupt, megalomaniacal used-car dealer backed by a private militia of deputized delinquents—ruthlessly seizes totalitarian control of the town, while Iraq War veteran Dale Barbara organizes a desperate resistance to uncover the dome's mysterious origins. Surrounded by toxic agricultural fires and rapidly suffocating under rising greenhouse gases, a brave band of beleaguered townspeople, including newspaper editor Julia Shumway, physician's assistant Rusty Everett, and a handful of local children, fight against time to prevent total catastrophic extermination.

On audio, *Under the Dome* delivers an unrelenting, claustrophobic adrenaline rush that functions like a prestige television thriller voiced directly into your ears. Narrator Raul Esparza commands the enormous ensemble cast with fiery kinetic momentum, making the rapid, terrifying breakdown of small-town civil order, the poisonous spread of propane hoarding, and Big Jim's pious, hypocritical villainy feel sickeningly immediate. King demonstrates his peerless ability to orchestrate multi-perspective chaos, showing how quickly civilized society can devolve into tribal savagery under environmental pressure. Narrator Soundcheck: Raul Esparza

Broadway veteran Raul Esparza delivers a high-octane, propulsive vocal performance that captures the mounting panic and suffocating claustrophobia of Chester’s Mill. Esparza gives chilling vocal distinction to Big Jim Rennie’s folksy, predatory sociopathy, while voicing Dale Barbara with weary, determined heroism. His breathless pacing sustains relentless tension during the town’s chaotic riots and explosive firefights across thirty-four hours. Cleanly recorded with dynamic acoustic range and clear dialogue separation, Esparza’s passionate narration does full justice to King’s relentless narrative drive.
