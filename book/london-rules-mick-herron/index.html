About this Audiobook

Savage Political Satire, Tabloid Treachery, and Whitehall Crossfire

Spanning eleven blistering hours of black comedy and deadly tradecraft, *London Rules* represents Mick Herron at the absolute height of his satirical powers. In the cynical lexicon of British intelligence, the unwritten 'London Rules' dictate that every operative's foremost priority is covering their own back, protecting their budget, and ensuring someone else takes the blame when operations collapse. When a series of seemingly motiveless, bizarre terrorist attacks strikes outside London—a village train derailed in Derbyshire, a penguin enclosure attacked at a zoo—First Desk Claude Whelan finds himself floundering under immense pressure from the populist Prime Minister and scheming MP Peter Judd.

Meanwhile, a malicious tabloid smear campaign threatens the political career of Judd’s rival, and Slough House's own obnoxious computer genius Roderick Ho discovers that his glamorous new girlfriend may actually be an assassin targeting him for elimination. As the slow horses attempt to protect Ho while investigating the provincial terror strikes, Jackson Lamb sniffs out a far more sinister plot orchestrated by foreign agents and domestic power-brokers designed to manipulate the British electorate. Herron weaves these disparate threads with effortless virtuosity, skewering Brexit-era political posturing, Whitehall incompetence, and tabloid journalism with venomous precision.

The pacing is immaculate, punctuated by sudden bursts of visceral violence and some of the funniest dialogue ever written in the espionage genre. Lamb's outrageous psychological torment of his staff reaches comic perfection, yet his operational cunning remains terrifyingly sharp. Sean Barrett’s narration breathes life into every grotesque political operator, panicked bureaucrat, and desperate spook. *London Rules* is a tour de force that cements the Slough House universe as modern crime fiction’s most entertaining satire.

Narrator Soundcheck: Sean Barrett

Sean Barrett’s reading of *London Rules* is pure auditory gold. His comedic timing with Roderick Ho’s delusional arrogance is hilarious, contrasting perfectly with Jackson Lamb’s phlegmatic, cigar-choked contempt. Barrett captures the slippery, calculated cadences of Whitehall politicians and the frantic, sweaty panic of First Desk Claude Whelan with exceptional vocal skill. In action sequences, his steady, resonant baritone conveys sudden lethality without sacrificing clarity, creating an unmissable eleven-hour listening experience.
