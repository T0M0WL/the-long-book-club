Fifteen Hours of Viral Small-Town Paranoia and Stitched-Shut Witchcraft in Modern Folk Horror

Welcome to Black Spring, a picturesque town tucked away in New York's Hudson Valley that appears on no tourist maps—because for three centuries, its residents have lived under the curse of Katherine van Wyler, the 17th-century Black Rock Witch. Appearing silently in living rooms and kitchens with her eyes and mouth sewn shut with thick thread, Katherine cannot be killed or driven out; the town survives only through strict neighborhood surveillance, smartphone apps tracking her whereabouts, and a taboo against outsiders ever learning the truth. Everything unravels when restless local teenagers decide to taunt the witch and upload footage online.

On audio, Thomas Olde Heuvelt's *Hex* fuses classic Folk Horror and Gothic curses with terrifying modern social satire. Hearing polite suburban parents enforce brutal quarantine protocols—while the constant dread of those stitched lips opening hangs in the air—creates claustrophobic suspense alongside Harvest Home and Slewfoot: A Tale of Bewitchery.

Clocking in at fourteen hours and thirty-six minutes for a single credit, *Hex* is darkly funny, merciless, and genuinely disturbing.

Narrator Soundcheck: Teri Schnaubelt (UK) / Jeff Harding (US)

Narrator Jeff Harding anchors all fourteen and a half hours with razor-sharp pacing and atmospheric control. Harding nails the creepy suburban banality of Black Spring's town council—contrasting bureaucratic paranoia and teenage bravado against sudden, skin-crawling bursts of supernatural terror with crisp acoustic polish. Note on regional editions: the Audible UK edition linked here is narrated by Teri Schnaubelt, while Audible US features Jeff Harding's edition.
