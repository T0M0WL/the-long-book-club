About this Audiobook

Three centuries after the Fall of Hyperion, condemned criminal Raul Endymion must rescue the messianic child Aenea from the Vatican Pax fleet.

Interstellar Resurrection, Papal Crusades, and Far-Future River Worlds

Spanning over twenty-three sweeping, philosophically daring hours, *Endymion* opens the monumental concluding duology of Dan Simmons’ legendary Hyperion Cantos, picking up centuries after the apocalyptic events of Hyperion and The Fall of Hyperion. Three hundred years have passed since the farsaster portals collapsed and the parasitic TechnoCore was cast out, leaving human civilization shattered. In the power vacuum, the Roman Catholic Church has emerged as the supreme galactic superpower—the Pax—offering humanity physical resurrection through the gruesome cruciform parasite in exchange for absolute spiritual and temporal obedience.

Enter Raul Endymion: a convicted murderer on the backwater world of Hyperion who is spared from execution by the enigmatic Martin Silenus for a singular mission. Guided by the ancient poet, Raul must enter the Time Tombs to intercept Aenea—the eleven-year-old daughter of Brawne Lamia and the John Keats cybrid—who is destined to become humanity's messiah and dismantle the Pax. Accompanied by the loyal blue-skinned android A. Bettik, Raul and Aenea embark on an extraordinary journey across the abandoned River Tethys, traveling through ancient stargates into exotic, alien worlds while pursued by Father Captain Federico de Soya's elite military starships and the terrifying, bladed metallic horror of the Shrike.

Simmons shifts the structural framework from the Canterbury-style anthology of the earlier books into a breathtaking picaresque interstellar chase novel, rich with Keatsian poetry, theological debate, and jaw-dropping planetary worldbuilding. As the fugitives navigate deadly fungal forests and crystalline mountains, Simmons explores the price of eternal life and the spiritual rot of dogmatic power. In audio, narrator Victor Bevine delivers a magnificent performance, imbuing Raul’s retrospective narration with raw humanity, humor, and tender romantic reverence for Aenea. It is a stunning, indispensable continuation for every devotee of golden-age Space Opera.

Narrator Soundcheck: Victor Bevine

Victor Bevine gives an extraordinary performance on Endymion, seamlessly taking the helm of Dan Simmons’ universe. Bevine grounds Raul Endymion’s first-person perspective in rugged, unpretentious warmth, making the narrator immediately relatable amidst mind-bending far-future science. He captures Father Captain de Soya’s conflicted piety and military resolve with nuanced solemnity, while voicing Aenea with eerie maturity and gentle wonder. Bevine’s rhythmic pacing across twenty-three hours keeps the interstellar chase riveting and acoustically immersive throughout.
