About this Audiobook

An unassuming MI6 desk officer handling African affairs leaks classified intelligence out of personal gratitude, triggering a deadly internal security inquest.

Graham Greene's Heartbreaking Masterpiece of Bureaucratic Treason and Love

Clocking in at ten and a half profoundly moving hours, *The Human Factor* is Graham Greene’s crowning espionage achievement, written late in his life with the melancholic wisdom, moral clarity, and intimate insider knowledge of a former MI6 officer. Greene, who served alongside notorious Soviet double agent Kim Philby in wartime intelligence, dismantles the glamorous mythology of the spy thriller to examine the quiet, mundane realities of bureaucratic desk jobs in Whitehall. Protagonist Maurice Castle is a gentle, sixty-two-year-old civil servant who oversees the South African sub-section, desiring nothing more than a peaceful commute home to Berkhamsted, a glass of whisky, and the company of his Black South African wife, Sarah, and their young son.

The novel’s tragedy begins when internal security detects a leak originating from Castle’s small, two-man office. Rather than conducting a thorough investigation, the ruthless service doctor and senior mandarins coldly decide to eliminate Castle’s innocent, gambling-addicted assistant Davis using an untraceable liver toxin. The bitter irony is that Castle is indeed the mole: not out of allegiance to Marxist dogma, but out of gratitude to a communist agent who helped Sarah escape the brutal clutches of the South African apartheid police years earlier. Listening in audio brings Greene’s poignant, understated dialogue to the fore as Castle finds himself trapped in a narrowing cage between his moral duty to his family and the unforgiving machinery of British state security.

For listeners seeking an emotionally devastating, intellectually profound exploration of Cold War espionage on a single credit, *The Human Factor* is an irreplaceable masterpiece. The late, celebrated actor Tim Pigott-Smith delivers a performance of breathtaking dignity and sorrow, capturing the tragic isolation of a good man crushed by geopolitical systems. Alongside fellow tradecraft landmarks like Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy and Slow Horses, it stands as an enduring classic of modern literature that will haunt your thoughts long after the final chapter concludes, offering unmatched emotional depth.

Narrator Soundcheck: Tim Pigott-Smith

Tim Pigott-Smith delivers a tour de force of understated British acting, imbuing Maurice Castle with quiet gentleness, weary exhaustion, and deep moral integrity. Pigott-Smith’s aristocratic baritone is warm and civilized, perfectly capturing the polite, chilling cruelty of senior MI6 interrogators who order assassinations over dry martinis at London clubs. His characterizations of Sarah and the innocent, doomed Davis are handled with delicate empathy, ensuring that Greene’s poignant philosophical themes hit home with profound emotional power across ten and a half hours.
