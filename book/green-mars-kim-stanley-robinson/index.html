About this Audiobook

Decades after the First Hundred's failed uprising, a native Martian generation emerges beneath a thickening sky, plotting an underground ecological and political revolution against Earth's transnational corporations.

Over Twenty-Seven Sweeping Hours Where Terraformed Ecology Collides with Corporate Empire

Picking up decades after the shattering rebellion that closed Red Mars, Hugo Award winner *Green Mars* widens the lens from the surviving First Hundred to the first native-born generation of Martians—children raised in hidden subterranean sanctuaries beneath the polar ice caps while Earth's transnational metanationals tighten their grip on the surface cities. As genetically engineered lichens, alpine mosses, and glacial meltwaters begin painting the crater floors emerald green, political resistance hardens into a daring subterranean coalition drafting a brand-new post-capitalist Martian society.

Listening to *Green Mars* in marathon audio lets Robinson’s extraordinary patience shine. Rather than rushing toward easy action clichés, the twenty-seven-hour runtime breathes with ecological wonder—tracking atmospheric pressure climbing millibar by millibar alongside secret diplomatic summits, radical underground economics, and thrilling high-altitude escapes across Valles Marineris. It sits right at the heart of our Solarpunk and Hard Sci-Fi catalog, showing how biological restoration and cooperative governance grow hand-in-hand under pressure—echoing the civic ingenuity of New York 2140 and Walkaway.

Spanning twenty-seven hours and ten minutes for a single Audible credit, *Green Mars* is a feast of worldbuilding that builds toward a breathtaking, planet-altering climax—setting the stage seamlessly for the colossal finale, Blue Mars.

Narrator Soundcheck: Richard Ferrone

Returning seamlessly from *Red Mars*, Richard Ferrone sustains all twenty-seven hours with rugged warmth, sharp political timing, and crisp clarity across Robinson's expanded cast of native Martian revolutionaries and aging First Hundred legends. Ferrone handles multi-generational shifts and technical terraforming terminology with calm authority, making the sweeping mid-trilogy revolution feel urgent and deeply lived-in.
