About this Audiobook

Humans and Portiid spiders venture into deep space to discover an ocean world ruled by hyper-intelligent octopuses and an ancient parasite.

We Are Going on an Adventure: Mel Hudson Makes Alien Consciousness Unforgettable

*Children of Ruin* expands Adrian Tchaikovsky’s Arthur C. Clarke Award-winning universe into astonishing new biological frontiers. Generations after humans and uplifted Portiid spiders established a fragile symbiotic civilization, a joint exploratory ark travels to the Nod system following ancient terraforming signals. What they encounter is an aquatic biosphere ruled by uplifted, hyper-curious octopuses whose decentralized consciousness operates across eight semi-autonomous limbs—and a far more terrifying, parasitic entity whose cheerful refrain, *“We’re going on an adventure!”*, masks apocalyptic biological horror.

Narrator Mel Hudson once again proves why she is the definitive voice of Tchaikovsky’s speculative fiction. Translating cephalopod intelligence—which communicates through chromatic skin patterns, emotional shifts, and sensory pulses—into spoken word is a monumental challenge, yet Hudson executes it with mesmerizing nuance. The tension between cautious spider logic, impulsive octopus curiosity, and the horrifying biological contagion creates an auditory thriller of the highest order. Fans who were enraptured by Children of Time or the cosmic intrigue of Hyperion will find themselves thoroughly absorbed.

At over fifteen hours of unrelenting creativity, this audiobook delivers magnificent credit value. It is rare for a sequel to match the originality of a landmark predecessor, but Tchaikovsky’s audacious evolutionary imagination and Hudson’s immaculate delivery make this an indispensable science fiction listen.

Narrator Soundcheck: Mel Hudson

Mel Hudson gives a bravura performance, maintaining pristine vocal consistency while voicing entirely disparate evolutionary physiologies. Her chilling delivery of the alien organism's disarming, infectious cheerfulness is skin-crawlingly effective. The audio production is clean, crisp, and expertly balanced between intimate biological monologues and sweeping deep-space horror.
