Twenty-Two Hours of Full-Cast Solarpunk Optimism Inside a Flooded SuperVenice Manhattan

After two catastrophic climate 'Pulses' raise global sea levels by fifty feet, Lower Manhattan is no longer dead—it is thriving as a watery SuperVenice where canal-boats weave between skyscrapers, intertidal farms cling to rooftops, and citizens cross Midtown on elevated skybridges. Inside the historic MetLife Tower cooperative, an unforgettable cast of New Yorkers—two irreverent coders, a sharp-eyed hedge fund trader, a fearless NYPD inspector, a charismatic wildlife streamer, and two orphaned canal-boat scavengers—find themselves racing to uncover missing gold bullion and stop predatory billionaires from privatizing their building.

On audio, Kim Stanley Robinson's *New York 2140* shines as a vibrant Full-Cast companion to The Ministry for the Future inside our Solarpunk and Science Fiction shelves. Having nine distinct voice actors bring each resident's streetwise Brooklyn or Midtown cadence to life turns an ambitious systemic novel into an infectious, big-hearted ensemble heist.

Clocking in at twenty-two hours and thirty-four minutes for a single credit, *New York 2140* proves that climate fiction can be funny, defiant, and wildly entertaining.
