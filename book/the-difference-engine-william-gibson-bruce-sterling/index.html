Fifteen Hours of the 1990 Alternate-History Classic That Birthed the Steampunk Genre

What if the information age arrived a century early, powered not by silicon microchips, but by coal smoke and spinning brass gears? In *The Difference Engine*—the 1990 landmark collaboration by cyberpunk pioneers William Gibson and Bruce Sterling—Lord Charles Babbage has triumphed in 1855 Britain. Steam-driven analytical engines crunch census data, control the stock market, and power a global British technocracy led by Lord Byron's Industrial Radical Party. Into this smog-choked London step courtesan Sybil Gerard, palaeontologist Edward Mallory, and diplomatic spy Laurence Oliphant, all hunting a lethal stack of punched brass cards known as the 'Modus.'

Experiencing *The Difference Engine* in audio transforms its dense Victorian period detail and mosaic structure into a gripping alternate-history espionage thriller. Anchoring the Origin Foundation slot on our Steampunk hub alongside its East Asian descendant Silkpunk, this nearly fifteen-hour recording shows the exact moment Cyberpunk looked backward into the nineteenth century.

Clocking in at fourteen hours and fifty-three minutes for a single credit, *The Difference Engine* is essential listening for anyone fascinated by the history of speculative fiction and Victorian technothrillers.

Narrator Soundcheck: Simon Vance

Audie Award-winning maestro Simon Vance brings effortless Victorian authenticity and brisk narrative drive to Gibson and Sterling's intricate world. Vance shifts seamlessly between aristocratic Whitehall salons, grimy London rookeries, and scientific lectures at the Royal Society, keeping every thread of the punch-card conspiracy clear across nearly fifteen hours.
