About this Audiobook

A murder in a tight-knit English amateur theatre troupe unravels entirely through emails, WhatsApp messages, and police transcripts. A masterfully produced 12-hour full-cast whodunnit that turns listeners into forensic investigators.

Janice Hallett’s 12-Hour Epistolary Whodunnit Brought to Life by a Stellar Full Cast

*The Appeal* brilliantly reinvents the classic English village mystery for the digital age. In the picturesque town of Lockwood, the Fairway Players amateur theatre group is rehearsing their upcoming production of *All My Sons* when tragedy strikes: two-year-old Poppy Reswick, granddaughter of the troupe’s matriarch, is diagnosed with a rare cancer. As an urgent charity appeal is launched to fund experimental treatment, an undercurrent of petty jealousies, financial anomalies, and passive-aggressive group emails escalates until someone is dead—and someone has been wrongly convicted.

Told entirely through modern epistolary artifacts—emails, WhatsApp threads, letters, and police evidence packets examined by two law students—this format could have been dry in print. In audio, however, a premier ensemble cast turns the Lockwood community into a riotous, voyeuristic auditory feast. Highlighted in our Best Long Cozy Crime Audiobooks collection and rivaling the immersive character dynamism found in Best Long Cinematic Audiobooks, Janice Hallett forces the listener to become the detective, sifting vocal inflections for deceit.

At twelve and a half hours of razor-sharp social satire and forensic clue-gathering, *The Appeal* offers exceptional return on a single Audible credit. The puzzle is watertight, rewarding attentive listeners with genuine eureka moments as seemingly trivial email sign-offs and forwarding chains suddenly expose alibis. It is a modern, endlessly inventive triumph of British audio drama.

Narrator Soundcheck: Gabrielle Glaister, David Timson

A large full-cast production can easily feel disjointed, but director Jessica Dromgoole orchestrates this sprawling company with razor-sharp clarity. Gabrielle Glaister and David Timson lead a versatile cast of British voice actors who capture the pitch-perfect suburban passive-aggression of English committee culture. Every actor nails the cadence of modern informal text—the self-serving patronising tone of Haydn Holderness, the quiet desperation of newcomer Samantha, and the chilly manipulations of the Hayward family. Audio cues keep navigation effortless.
