About this Audiobook

The sweeping prequel to The Killer Angels traces the opening years of the American Civil War—from Fort Sumter and Manassas to Antietam, Fredericksburg, and Chancellorsville—through the eyes of Robert E. Lee, Stonewall Jackson, Winfield Scott Hancock, and Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain.

Over Twenty-Two Sweeping Hours Charting the Road from Manassas to Chancellorsville

Written by Jeff Shaara as the chronological prequel to his father Michael Shaara’s Pulitzer-winning *The Killer Angels*, *Gods and Generals* opens the curtain on the first two agonizing years of the American Civil War. Moving fluidly across both sides of the conflict—from First Manassas and Williamsburg to the carnage of Antietam, the frozen hillside of Fredericksburg, and the fateful woods of Chancellorsville—the novel anchors its sprawling Military History in four unforgettable men: Robert E. Lee, Stonewall Jackson, Winfield Scott Hancock, and Bowdoin college professor Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain.

Shaara’s gift is stripping away textbook marble to reveal living, breathing commanders wrestling with duty, faith, and unbearable loss. Hearing Chamberlain trade Latin lectures for a Maine regiment, or watching Jackson’s devout intensity collide with Lee’s gentlemanly burden of command, makes every tactical march feel deeply personal. Listeners who loved the political stakes of Team of Rivals or Ron Chernow’s Grant will find *Gods and Generals* an irresistible Historical Fiction bridge straight into The Killer Angels.

At twenty-two hours and thirty-eight minutes for a single Audible credit, Book 1 of *The Civil War Trilogy* delivers extraordinary scale and emotional momentum.

Narrator Soundcheck: Mark Bramhall

Veteran narrator Mark Bramhall commands over twenty-two hours with crisp clarity, warm gravitas, and zero fatigue. He differentiates the four central commanders cleanly—giving Chamberlain a thoughtful New England cadence, Lee a measured Virginia restraint, and Jackson an intense, clipped resolve—so sprawling multi-corps engagements stay vivid and effortless to follow purely by ear.
