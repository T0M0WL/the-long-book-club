About this Audiobook

Religious turmoil, espionage, and the Spanish Armada threaten Elizabethan England in this thirty-hour Kingsbridge epic.

Tudor Espionage on an Epic Scale: John Lee Powers 30 Hours of Religious Intrigue

In *A Column of Fire*, Ken Follett propels the Kingsbridge saga into the turbulent Elizabethan era of 1558, where the battleground shifts from cathedral architecture to clandestine intelligence networks and religious wars. As Queen Elizabeth I ascends the English throne surrounded by Catholic conspirators plotting her assassination, Ned Willard of Kingsbridge joins the Queen's master of spies, Sir Francis Walsingham, to establish England's first foreign secret service. Ned’s loyalty to the crown pits him directly against his lifelong love, Margery Fitzgerald, whose devout Catholic family is drawn into treasonous plots stretching from Paris safehouses to the Spanish Armada.

John Lee narrates this thirty-hour epic with magnificent gravitas and theatrical flair. The expansion of Kingsbridge’s horizons across Europe—taking in the St. Bartholomew’s Day Massacre in Paris, the imprisonment of Mary, Queen of Scots, and the thunderous naval clashes in the English Channel—is executed with seamless historical detail. Paired with companion classics like The Pillars of the Earth or cold-war mole hunts like Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy, this novel reveals the foundational origins of modern counterintelligence.

With over thirty hours of continuous suspense for a single monthly token, *A Column of Fire* represents extraordinary listening value. It bridges romance, cloak-and-dagger statecraft, and massive military confrontations with breathtaking narrative momentum.
