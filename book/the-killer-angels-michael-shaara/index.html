About this Audiobook

Winner of the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction, Michael Shaara's landmark novel recreates the four decisive days of the Battle of Gettysburg through the intimate thoughts, doubts, and courage of Robert E. Lee, James Longstreet, Joshua Chamberlain, and John Buford.

Nearly Fourteen Unforgettable Hours Inside the Four Days That Decided Gettysburg

Winner of the 1975 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction and widely hailed as the finest American Civil War novel ever written, Michael Shaara’s *The Killer Angels* narrows the entire fate of a fractured nation down to four blistering July days in 1863 around a quiet Pennsylvania crossroads called Gettysburg. Told intimately through the eyes of the officers on both ridges—a weary, heartsick Robert E. Lee, a skeptical James Longstreet pleading against a frontal assault, Union cavalryman John Buford holding the high ground, and Colonel Joshua Lawrence Chamberlain defending Little Round Top with bayonets fixed—every chapter pulses with tragic humanity.

What elevates *The Killer Angels* into timeless Classics territory is how cleanly it translates complex Military History into pure human drama. Even without a battlefield map in hand, you feel the heat, the dust, the friendship fractured between Union and Confederate officers who once served together, and the heartbreaking inevitability of Pickett’s Charge. Serving as the luminous centerpiece between Jeff Shaara’s Gods and Generals and The Last Full Measure, it is essential American History listening.

At thirteen hours and forty-four minutes for a single Audible credit, *The Killer Angels* is lean, thunderous, and endlessly relistenable.

Narrator Soundcheck: Stephen Hoye

Stephen Hoye delivers a taut, deeply resonant nearly fourteen-hour performance worthy of a Pulitzer classic. Balancing hushed midnight strategy tents with the chaotic roar of Little Round Top and Cemetery Ridge, Hoye nails the quiet dignity of Longstreet's foreboding and the quiet heroism of Chamberlain's Maine volunteers without ever tipping into theatrical excess.
