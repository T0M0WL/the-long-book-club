About this Audiobook

A wealthy aristocrat serving on a jury discovers the prostitute on trial for murder is the innocent girl he seduced years earlier, sparking a harrowing quest for redemption across Siberia.

Tolstoy’s Scathing, 19-Hour Moral Masterpiece of Redemption and Siberian Exile

Spanning nineteen and a half gripping hours, *Resurrection* was the final full-length novel published during Leo Tolstoy’s lifetime, outselling both War and Peace and Anna Karenina in Russia upon its sensational release in 1899. The story begins in a suffocating Moscow courtroom where Prince Dmitri Nekhlyudov, an idle aristocrat, sits on a criminal jury. To his horror, he recognizes the young prostitute accused of poisoning a merchant: she is Katusha Maslova, the pure, sweet peasant ward his family raised, whom Nekhlyudov seduced and abandoned ten years earlier, inadvertently condemning her to social ruin and destitution.

When a grotesque clerical error by the indifferent jury mistakenly sentences Katusha to four years of hard labor in Siberia, Nekhlyudov is seized by an overwhelming spiritual crisis. Desperate to expiate his guilt, he renounces his estate, surrenders his inheritance to his peasants, and follows Katusha on foot along the brutal prisoner transport trail to Siberia. Tolstoy uses their grueling physical and moral pilgrimage to launch an incandescent critique of Tsarist prisons, corrupt state religion, judicial callousness, and systemic peasant oppression. The audio format turns this moral crusade into an extraordinarily intimate drama, transforming Nekhlyudov’s inner spiritual awakening into an emotionally gripping listen.

For classic literature lovers who have already experienced the grand battlefields of War and Peace and the tragic salons of Anna Karenina, *Resurrection* is the essential third pillar of Tolstoy's genius. Malk Williams’s resonant, empathetic narration lends every courtroom argument and Siberian penal convoy scene immense dignity and sorrow. It stands as a brilliant capstone to Classics that offers profound emotional catharsis and unbeatable value for a single monthly credit.

Narrator Soundcheck: Malk Williams

Malk Williams delivers a dignified, resonant performance that perfectly captures the moral weight and passionate social conscience of late-period Tolstoy. Williams handles Nekhlyudov’s agonizing journey from cynical aristocratic privilege to humble spiritual clarity with profound psychological conviction, while giving Katusha Maslova a defiant, tragic pride that avoids easy sentimentality. His stately pacing and nuanced characterizations of prison wardens, political dissidents, and Siberian exiles make nineteen hours deeply engaging from beginning to end.
