About this Audiobook

As the names of disgraced MI5 agents are wiped from official databases, Russian hit squads begin assassinating slow horses across London.

Digital Erasure, Russian Wet-Work, and Guerilla Espionage in London

Clocking in at just under ten electrifying hours, *Slough House*—the self-titled seventh volume in Mick Herron’s acclaimed series—delivers a masterclass in modern political paranoia, hybrid warfare, and cold-blooded tradecraft. In the wake of a botched novichok-style poisoning on British soil, diplomatic tensions between London and Moscow have reached a boiling point. But beneath the public bluster, Whitehall is eager to placate Russian oligarchs and restore trade ties. To clear the diplomatic deck, a sinister bureaucratic decree is enacted: the slow horses are formally purged from the service's digital archives, their security clearances revoked, their existence quietly scrubbed from history.

Simultaneously, a shadowy Russian assassination team begins hunting down former Slough House operatives, staging their deaths as tragic accidents, suicides, and muggings gone wrong. As paranoia sweeps through the remaining rejects, River Cartwright, Catherine Standish, and Louisa Guy realize they have been abandoned by their own government as expendable collateral damage. Enter Jackson Lamb: enraged by the violation of his sacred rule—that only *he* is allowed to torment his people—Lamb unleashes his lethal Cold War instincts in a vicious, asymmetrical guerrilla counter-offensive across London.

Herron skewers the contemporary intersection of cyber warfare, troll farms, and corrupt political expedience with surgical brilliance. The pacing is breathless, matching the urgency of an unsparing manhunt where every shadow hides a suppressed pistol. In audio, Sean Barrett’s veteran gravel and razor-sharp delivery accentuate the claustrophobic dread and caustic wit. For listeners seeking a spy thriller that reflects the chilling realities of modern hybrid conflict while delivering world-class entertainment, *Slough House* is an absolute triumph.

Narrator Soundcheck: Sean Barrett

Sean Barrett’s performance on *Slough House* is dark, menacing, and darkly hilarious. His gravelly baritone captures the damp, grey London atmosphere with palpable realism. Barrett’s Jackson Lamb is in peak form: savage, wheezing, and radiating an understated menace that reminds listeners why foreign intelligence services fear him above all others. He handles the tense street encounters and close-quarters combat scenes with masterly pacing, ensuring the high-stakes narrative never loses its tight grip on the listener.
