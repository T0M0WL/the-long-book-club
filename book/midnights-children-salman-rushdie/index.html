About this Audiobook

Born at the exact stroke of midnight on August 15, 1947—the moment of India's independence—Saleem Sinai discovers he is telepathically linked to a thousand other children born in that same fateful hour, his life inextricably handcuffed to his nation's modern history.

Salman Rushdie’s Booker of Bookers Masterpiece of India's Independence in Spectacular Audio

Salman Rushdie’s landmark novel did not merely win the 1981 Booker Prize; it was twice crowned the 'Booker of Bookers,' celebrated as the single greatest work of fiction in the award's entire history. Born at the exact stroke of midnight on August 15, 1947—the precise moment India achieved independence from the British Empire—protagonist Saleem Sinai finds himself handcuffed to history. Blessed with a cavernous, telepathic nose that functions as a radio receiver connecting him to the 1,001 other children born across the nation during that first magical hour, Saleem recounts his tumultuous life story from a Bombay pickle factory, blending personal tragedy with the birth, partition, wars, and political turmoil of modern South Asia.

In its unabridged twenty-six-hour audio incarnation, *Midnight's Children* reveals its true, intended form as an exuberant oral performance. Rushdie’s prose—overflowing with tumbling street slang, culinary metaphors, multilingual puns, and mythic digressions—can occasionally intimidate silent readers on the physical page, but spoken aloud to an attentive listener, it crackles with theatrical vibrancy. Showcased as a crowning jewel of our Magical Realism Collection and standing alongside grand historical sagas in our Best Fiction Audiobooks catalog, this recording turns the turbulent history of the subcontinent into an unforgettable, noisy family epic.

Clocking in at nearly twenty-six hours of linguistic fireworks, this release delivers extraordinary credit-to-hour value for lovers of ambitious global literature. Rushdie weaves the magical realism of One Hundred Years of Solitude with the biting political satire of modern India, creating an expansive canvas where myth and reality collide with relentless energy. Narrator Soundcheck: Homer Todiwala

Homer Todiwala gives an astounding, vivacious performance that brings Rushdie’s kaleidoscopic prose and polyphonic character roster to brilliant theatrical life. His natural grasp of Urdu, Hindi, and Indian English cadences infuses Saleem’s rambling recollections with charming warmth, sardonic wit, and deep pathos. Todiwala handles rapid-fire dialogue, bombastic street banter, and quiet confessional intimacies with effortless vocal stamina across all twenty-six hours. Pristine audio engineering and warm acoustic balance ensure that every lyrical alliteration, tongue-in-cheek insult, and cultural nuance lands with maximum dramatic and comedic impact.
