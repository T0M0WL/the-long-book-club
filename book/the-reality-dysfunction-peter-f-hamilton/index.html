About this Audiobook

In the twenty-seventh century, an unexpected rift in reality on a frontier planet allows the souls of the dead to return, threatening humanity's galactic empire.

The Grand 41-Hour Space Opera That Stretches the Boundaries of Imagination

Spanning forty-one staggering hours, *The Reality Dysfunction* is the monumental opening salvo of Peter F. Hamilton’s legendary Night’s Dawn Trilogy, widely regarded as one of the most ambitious space operas in modern science fiction. Set in the 26th century, humanity has expanded across thousands of star systems divided into two rival factions: the Adamists, who embrace cybernetics, neural nanotechnology, and mechanized starships; and the Edenists, who utilize bio-engineered 'bitecology,' living habitats, and telepathic voidships born from sentient gene-spliced organisms.

The fragile political balance of the Confederation is shattered when an occult ritual conducted by an abused telepath on the frontier planet Lalonde tears an incomprehensible tear in the fabric of space-time. Through this breach comes the 'Possessed'—the tormented, desperate consciousnesses of the human dead seeking to return and claim living bodies across the stars. Hamilton brilliantly blends hard science fiction, biological nanotechnology, intergalactic dogfights, and existential horror across an immense canvas of viewpoints, tracking rogue starship captain Joshua Calvert, Edenist spacer Syrinx, and planetary resistance fighters struggling to comprehend an enemy that defies physics.

For audiobook listeners who desire sheer cosmic vastness, deep scientific worldbuilding, and unmatched credit value, *The Reality Dysfunction* is an undisputed heavyweight. John Lee’s commanding, resonant performance anchors forty-one hours of interstellar conflict with unmatched authority and dynamic propulsion. Standing proudly alongside Hamilton’s Pandora's Star and Dan Simmons's Hyperion, this audio odyssey defines the summit of Space Opera listening.

Narrator Soundcheck: John Lee

John Lee delivers a powerhouse performance that matches the sheer scale and intellectual ambition of Hamilton’s twenty-seventh-century universe. Lee effortlessly switches between the laid-back, charming swagger of Joshua Calvert, the clinical authority of Edenist hive minds, and the bloodcurdling, raspy malevolence of the Possessed. His crisp enunciation of complex theoretical physics and biological terminology keeps forty-one hours moving at a ferocious clip, ensuring every starship skirmish and planetary invasion feels monumental.
