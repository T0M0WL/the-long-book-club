Over Twenty-One Luminous Hours Unlocking Modernism's Greatest Meditation on Memory

Few books cast a longer shadow over twentieth-century Modernist literature than *Swann's Way*, the unforgettable opening volume of Marcel Proust's *In Search of Lost Time*. Triggered by the famous taste of a tea-soaked madeleine, Proust's narrator unlocks an astonishingly vivid world: restless childhood bedtimes in provincial Combray, hawthorn-lined country walks, the glittering snobbery of Belle Époque Paris salons, and the intoxicating, agonizing jealousy of Charles Swann's love affair with the elusive Odette de Crécy.

Here is the great secret of reading Proust: his famously winding, clause-layered sentences were practically born to be heard aloud. Where your eyes can lose their footing on the printed page, a great narrator carries you across every musical rise and fall, turning dense Philosophical Fiction into an intimate, witty, deeply moving conversation. Paired with Ulysses and The Magic Mountain, it is an essential pillar of unabridged Classics audio.

At twenty-one hours and thirty-three minutes for a single Audible credit, *Swann's Way* turns literature's most intimidating mountain into a pure sensory pleasure.

Narrator Soundcheck: Neville Jason

Neville Jason's acclaimed Naxos recording of Proust is widely considered one of the crowning achievements in classic audiobook narration. Across twenty-one and a half hours, his warm, unhurried diction and subtle comic phrasing untangle Proust's longest sentences effortlessly—letting every flash of social satire, nostalgia, and heartache shimmer.
