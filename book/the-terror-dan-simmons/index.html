About this Audiobook

The men on board the HMS Terror have every right to be scared: their ship is stuck in the Arctic ice, rations are low, and there is a thing out there in the dark that is stalking them. Based on the true story of the lost Franklin Expedition.

Dan Simmons’s Chilling Twenty-Eight-Hour Arctic Survival Nightmare and Historical Horror in Audio

In the summer of 1845, two technologically advanced Royal Navy bomb vessels—HMS *Erebus* and HMS *Terror*—sail into the frozen uncharted waters of the Canadian Arctic seeking the legendary Northwest Passage. Commanded by Sir John Franklin and Captain Francis Crozier, the expedition becomes hopelessly trapped in the grinding pack ice of Victoria Strait, where endless polar winters, lead poisoning from faulty tinned provisions, and creeping scurvy decimate the crews. Worse still, across the pitch-black howling blizzard wastes, a monstrous, supernatural creature of pure predatory ice—a terrifying mythological entity resembling an immense polar bear—stalks the stranded sailors one by one. Aided by a mute Inuit woman the sailors name Lady Silence, Captain Crozier must lead over a hundred starving men on an agonizing sledge retreat across thousands of miles of hummocked ice, where freezing winds and cannibalistic despair await.

In its audio format, *The Terror* is an astonishingly atmospheric listening experience that will make you physically shiver beneath your headphones. Narrator Tom Sellwood’s clipped, stoic British delivery captures the stiff-upper-lip dignity of 19th-century naval officers as starvation, freezing temperatures, and madness strip away the veneer of Victorian empire. Positioned as a chilling titan in our Horror and Historical Fiction collections alongside Carrion Comfort and The Pillars of the Earth, this twenty-eight-hour recording turns maritime exploration into an agonizing, sublime auditory ordeal that surrounds you completely.

Simmons masterfully reconstructs the nautical vernacular, mechanical realities, and Inuit mythologies of the doomed Franklin expedition, creating an unforgettable portrait of human endurance against hostile nature. Narrator Soundcheck: Tom Sellwood

Narrator Tom Sellwood turns in a magnificent, pitch-perfect performance that feels completely authentic to the 1840s Royal Navy. Sellwood brings weary, melancholic gravitas to Captain Francis Crozier’s alcohol-clouded leadership, contrasting it brilliantly against the haughty optimism of Sir John Franklin and the menacing, mutinous whispers of Cornelius Hickey. His deliberate, patient cadence amplifies the suffocating silence of the Arctic pack ice and the terrifying, sudden violence of the creature. Mastered with pristine acoustic warmth and steady atmospheric dread throughout, this twenty-eight-hour recording is exceptional.
