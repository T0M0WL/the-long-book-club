About this Audiobook

Three centuries after the Final Empire, lawman Waxillium Ladrian returns from the frontier Roughs to the gaslit streets of Elendel to solve high-stakes robberies.

Six-Shooters, Steelpushing, and the Gaslamp Evolution of the Scadrian Cosmere

Spanning nearly eleven breathless, high-octane hours, *The Alloy of Law* marks Brandon Sanderson’s triumphant return to Scadrial, leaping three centuries past the apocalyptic conclusion of The Hero of Ages. Scadrial has entered a vibrant industrial era of steam trains, electric lights, and Colt-style revolvers. Yet the sacred Allomancy and Feruchemy of The Final Empire have not vanished; they have evolved into 'Twinborn' combinations that produce devastating combat synergies.

Enter Waxillium Ladrian: a nobleman who spent twenty years dispensing frontier justice in the arid Roughs as a Steel Crasher—able to push metal through the air while manipulating his own physical weight. When family tragedy forces Wax back to the capital of Elendel to assume his aristocratic duties, he is dragged into an underworld investigation involving 'the Vanishers'—a ruthless gang hijacking luxury cargo trains and kidnapping noblewomen. Partnering with his irrepressible, disguise-loving deputy Wayne and the brilliant aspiring magistrate Marasi Colms, Wax unleashes a storm of gunplay and kinetic magic across Elendel's skyline.

Narrator Michael Kramer delivers a magnificent performance, effortlessly capturing Wax’s weary gravitas and Wayne’s chameleon-like cockney accents and comic timing. The chemistry between the leads crackles with infectious warmth, offering a refreshing blend of Sherlock Holmes mystery and spaghetti western grit. For Cosmere devotees and newcomers alike, this fast-paced audio adventure provides unbeatable entertainment value on a single Audible credit.

Narrator Soundcheck: Michael Kramer

Michael Kramer’s narration on The Alloy of Law is an acoustic masterclass in comedic timing and kinetic action. Kramer inhabits Waxillium Ladrian with the gravelly, stoic authority of a classic western lawman, contrasting him brilliantly against Wayne’s hilarious, rapid-fire accents and eccentric disguises. His delivery of Sanderson’s complex physics-based gunplay is razor-sharp, keeping every ricochet and bullet-push perfectly intelligible. Pristinely produced, this nearly eleven-hour listen is pure Cosmere joy from start to finish.
