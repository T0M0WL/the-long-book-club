About this Audiobook

The Industrial Revolution and Napoleonic Wars collide in Kingsbridge as textile workers battle steam mechanization and the Battle of Waterloo.

Revolution, Steam, and Waterloo: John Lee Concludes the Kingsbridge Cycle with Majesty

In *The Armor of Light*, Ken Follett brings the monumental Kingsbridge series into the dawn of the modern world. Set between 1792 and 1815, the novel chronicles the violent arrival of the Industrial Revolution against the backdrop of the Napoleonic Wars. In Kingsbridge, the invention of the spinning jenny and power looms makes mill owners fabulously wealthy while throwing skilled weavers and destitute families into starvation. As corrupt magistrates outlaw trade unions under fear of French Jacobin radicalism, a generation of brave cloth workers, reformist magistrates, and courageous young soldiers must fight for human dignity at home while facing Napoleon’s cannons abroad.

John Lee once again proves why his voice is the heartbeat of Ken Follett’s audiobooks. He handles the regional West Country accents of weavers with authentic warmth while capturing the terrifying chaos of the Battle of Waterloo with breathtaking military precision. Follett excels at personalizing massive historical shifts through intimate human dramas of forbidden love, social climbing, and legal injustice. Alongside classic social panoramas like Les Misérables and War and Peace, this is historical storytelling at its most compelling.

At nearly twenty-two hours for a single Audible credit, *The Armor of Light* is a richly rewarding listening experience. It concludes a five-volume, millennium-spanning saga with heart, historical veracity, and profound emotional satisfaction.
