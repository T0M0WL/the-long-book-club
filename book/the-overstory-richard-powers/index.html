About this Audiobook

Nine seemingly unconnected strangers—from an Air Force loadmaster saved by a banyan tree to a hearing-impaired botanist—are drawn together by the ancient intelligence of trees to mount a desperate last stand for America's old-growth forests.

Twenty-Three Pulitzer-Winning Hours Revealing the Secret, Interlocking Intelligence of Forests

Winner of the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction, Richard Powers’s *The Overstory* is a towering literary symphony structured like a tree itself—from Roots and Trunk to Crown and Seeds. Opening with nine dazzling, standalone portraits of wildly different Americans—an Iowa farm family photographing a single chestnut tree across a century, a Vietnam War pilot saved mid-fall by a banyan, a hearing-impaired botanist discovering that trees communicate through underground fungal networks, a software pioneer building virtual worlds—the novel gradually braids their lives into an urgent, unforgettable stand to save the last ancient redwoods of the Pacific Northwest.

Experiencing *The Overstory* on audio is a genuine revelation. Powers’s musical, incantatory prose—filled with botanical wonder, courtroom tension, and quiet ecological awe—unfolds effortlessly when spoken aloud, turning complex forest biology into something thrillingly intimate and deeply moving. Sitting at the lyrical crossroads of Literary Fiction and modern Solarpunk activism alongside The Ministry for the Future and A Half-Built Garden, it permanently changes the way you look at every park, woodland trail, and street-side oak you walk past while listening.

Spanning twenty-two hours and fifty-eight minutes for a single Audible credit, *The Overstory* offers rare literary depth and astonishing staying power—an immersive eco-epic that lingers in your imagination long after the final hour fades.

Narrator Soundcheck: Suzanne Toren

Veteran narrator Suzanne Toren delivers an extraordinary twenty-three-hour tour de force, keeping nine deeply distinct protagonists—spanning Midwestern farmers, college radicals, brilliant scientists, and weary activists—vividly differentiated and emotionally grounded. Toren handles Powers's lyrical botanical prose and scientific vocabulary with musical warmth, measured cadence, and heartbreaking sincerity, turning a complex literary mosaic into a seamless, deeply absorbing listen.
