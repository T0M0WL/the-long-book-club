About this Audiobook

When an ice-hauler crew stumbles across an abandoned derelict and a cynical detective investigates a missing heiress on Ceres, a solar-system-wide conspiracy erupts into interstellar war.

The Masterful Hard-Boiled Space Epic That Rebuilt the Modern Genre

At an impressive twenty-one hours, *Leviathan Wakes* stands as the monumental launchpad for the nine-volume Expanse saga, delivering the most grounded, physically authentic space opera of the twenty-first century. Rather than relying on hyperdrives, magical shields, and artificial gravity plates, James S.A. Corey constructs a colonized solar system bound by strict Newtonian physics, cold corporate greed, and brutal high-g maneuvers. You can feel the bone-crushing decelerations, smell the recycled hydroponic air of Ceres Station, and hear the creaking hull plates of the ice freighter *Canterbury* and the gunship *Rocinante*. It avoids the sterile, detached exposition common to grand-scale science fiction by filtering colossal geopolitical turmoil through two deeply human, contrasting viewpoints that hook you from the very first distress signal.

The narrative alternates between Joe Miller, a cynical, rumpled Belter detective investigating the disappearance of a wealthy heiress across the grimy slums of asteroid settlements, and Jim Holden, a relentlessly idealistic ship officer whose insistence on broadcasting uncomfortable truths inadvertently triggers a three-way interplanetary cold war. Listening in audio brings an electric intimacy to this political powder keg. As the two men converge upon Eros Station to confront an extradimensional biological horror that threatens all biological life, the audio format allows Corey’s layered worldbuilding—from resource scarcity in the Asteroid Belt to Martian militarism—to unfold with the pulse-pounding urgency of a prestige television drama.

For audiobook listeners seeking maximum narrative mileage from a single monthly credit, this opening salvo is the ultimate gateway into a sweeping two-hundred-hour commitment. Jefferson Mays delivers one of the most celebrated vocal masterclasses in contemporary audio publishing, effortlessly carrying the sprawling cast across decades of conflict. Paired with companion space epics like Pandora's Star and Shards of Earth, it proves that grand Space Opera can be gritty, character-driven, and endlessly addictive, offering astronomical listening value that will keep you thoroughly captivated across weeks of daily commuting.

Narrator Soundcheck: Jefferson Mays

Jefferson Mays sets the gold standard for long-form science fiction performance. Tackling a stratified solar system, his greatest technical triumph is the creation of an organic Belter creole that feels entirely lived-in rather than theatrical. Mays contrasts Detective Miller’s weary, bourbon-soaked rasp against Jim Holden’s earnest Midwestern cadence with effortless dexterity. His deliberate, measured pacing ratchets up unbearable tension during close-quarters torpedo exchanges and body-horror encounters, turning twenty-one hours of intricate interplanetary intrigue into an unputdownable auditory thrill ride.
