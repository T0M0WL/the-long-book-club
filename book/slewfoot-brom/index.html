Slewfoot: A Tale of Bewitchery

Author: Brom | Narrator: Barrie Kreinik

Runtime: 14h 11m | Genre: Folk Horror, Horror, Historical Fiction, Fantasy, Witches

About this Audiobook

In 1666 Connecticut, young English widow Abitha fights to save her farm from her pious Puritan brother-in-law by forging a desperate pact with an ancient, horned woodland spirit awakening in the dark forest.

Fourteen Hours of Visceral New England Folk Horror and Pagan Vengeance

In 1666, in the shadow of the untamed Connecticut wilderness, spirited English bride Abitha is abruptly widowed when her husband dies under mysterious circumstances. Left at the mercy of her ruthless, debt-ridden brother-in-law Wallace—who plots to seize her land and sell her into indentured servitude—Abitha is ostracized by the pious Puritan village of Sutton. Deep in the surrounding dark woods, however, an ancient, horned nature spirit known to the locals as 'Slewfoot' has awakened hungry, confused, and covered in blood. Together, the desperate widow and the forgotten god forge a dangerous bond that will test the boundary between survival and damnation.

On audio, Brom's *Slewfoot* transforms from a lavishly illustrated dark folktale into a pulse-pounding, atmospheric audio drama. Hearing the suffocating, scripture-quoting menace of the Puritan elders clash against the wild, elemental whispers of the forest spirits creates an intoxicating sense of dread and rebellion. Featured on our Folk Horror hub alongside Harvest Home and Blackwater: The Complete Saga, this fourteen-hour standalone is tailor-made for autumn headphone listening.

Spanning fourteen hours and eleven minutes for a single credit, *Slewfoot* delivers a fiercely satisfying arc of female rage, ancient mythology, and folk horror catharsis.

Narrator Soundcheck: Barrie Kreinik

Barrie Kreinik gives an electric, deeply immersive performance across fourteen hours. She voices Abitha's transformation from a grieving, cornered outcast into a formidable force of nature with raw emotional conviction, while lending Samson/Slewfoot a resonant, otherworldly timbre and the Puritan villagers a chilling, self-righteous edge. Pristine audio production keeps every whispered incantation vivid.
