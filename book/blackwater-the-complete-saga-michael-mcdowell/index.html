About this Audiobook

After the black waters of the Perdido River flood the sawmill town of Elinor, Alabama, on Easter morning in 1919, the enigmatic Elinor Dammert emerges from a flooded hotel room to marry into the town's wealthiest timber dynasty.

Thirty Hours of Spellbinding Southern Gothic Folk Horror and Matriarchal Intrigue

Originally published in six monthly serialized volumes in 1983—a direct nod to Charles Dickens—Michael McDowell's *Blackwater* is one of the greatest cult masterpieces in American horror. On Easter Sunday in 1919, as the murky black waters of the Perdido River recede from the flooded streets of Elinor, Alabama, town councilman Oscar Caskey rescues a silent, copper-haired stranger named Elinor Dammert from an upstairs hotel room. Nobody knows how she survived four days without food or water, why she carries so little luggage, or why she is so eager to marry into the Caskey timber fortune and reshape their family across the next fifty years.

Experiencing all six volumes in this single thirty-hour audiobook omnibus is pure Southern Gothic magic. Rather than relying on cheap jump scares, McDowell weaves slow-burn family drama—boardroom rivalries, levee engineering, marriages, and inheritances—with sudden, chilling glimpses of what lurks beneath the Perdido River's surface. Anchoring our Folk Horror hub alongside Slewfoot: A Tale of Bewitchery, this marathon listen feels like sitting on a humid Alabama porch while a master storyteller spins a fifty-year dark fairy tale.

At thirty hours and fifteen minutes for a single Audible credit, *Blackwater: The Complete Saga* offers extraordinary six-books-in-one value. Once you meet the Caskey matriarchs and hear the river rising against the levee, thirty hours will fly by in a heartbeat.

Narrator Soundcheck: Matt Godfrey

Matt Godfrey delivers a career-defining narration across thirty hours, capturing the polite, venomous cadence of small-town Southern aristocracy with pitch-perfect precision. From the domineering Mary-Love Caskey to the eerie, unflappable calm of Elinor Dammert, Godfrey's subtle vocal characterizations and unhurried pacing make both the domestic comedy and the sudden visceral horror land with maximum impact.
