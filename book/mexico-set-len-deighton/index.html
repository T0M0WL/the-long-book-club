About this Audiobook

Reeling from his wife's catastrophic defection, cynical MI6 field agent Bernard Samson is tasked with orchestrating the defection of a KGB major.

Mexican Sun, Soviet Shadow Games, and Whitehall Paranoia

Spanning thirteen and a half engrossing hours of dry British wit and razor-sharp Cold War tradecraft, *Mexico Set* is the brilliant second volume of Len Deighton’s acclaimed 'Game, Set and Match' trilogy, following the shocking finale of Berlin Game. Protagonist Bernard Samson—a cynical, working-class MI6 intelligence officer raised in post-war Berlin—is still reeling from the devastating revelation that his own brilliant wife, Fiona, was a deep-cover KGB colonel who betrayed British intelligence and fled behind the Iron Curtain.

Rather than being granted time to grieve, Samson finds himself targeted by paranoid, upper-class Whitehall bureaucrats who suspect he was complicit in Fiona's treachery. To prove his loyalty, Samson is assigned an excruciatingly delicate, off-the-books assignment: make contact in Mexico City with Erich Stinnes, the KGB officer who ran Fiona’s network, and persuade him to defect to the West. What follows is a magnificent cat-and-mouse game across sun-drenched Mexican plazas, grimy London pubs, and snowy East German safehouses, as Samson dodges Soviet assassination teams while playing an intricate psychological game against both his quarry and his own treacherous superiors.

Deighton’s dialogue is a masterclass in realistic, world-weary tradecraft and caustic British sarcasm, contrasting the gritty reality of field work with the comfortable cowardice of senior mandarins. Samson's inner commentary on English social hierarchy, crumbling imperial arrogance, and personal loneliness gives the espionage procedural genuine literary weight. In audio, narrator James Lailey embodies Bernard Samson with perfection, delivering Samson's deadpan gallows humor and bruised emotional core with authentic London realism. For fans of John le Carré’s Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy and Mick Herron’s Slow Horses, *Mexico Set* is an indispensable British espionage masterpiece.

Narrator Soundcheck: James Lailey

James Lailey’s performance on Mexico Set is a triumphant continuation of Len Deighton’s espionage world. Lailey captures Bernard Samson’s laconic, cynical London baritone with effortless authenticity, letting Samson's dry gallows humor land with pinpoint comic timing. His rendering of KGB major Erich Stinnes carries subtle Eastern European menace and nervous calculation, while his upper-class Whitehall mandarins drip with snide bureaucratic cowardice. The sound engineering is crisp and atmospheric, making all thirteen hours a delight for spy enthusiasts.
