About this Audiobook

In the frozen wilderness of medieval northern Rus', wild-hearted Vasilisa alone can see the ancient household spirits protecting her village—spirits that begin to wither when a devout stepmother and fanatical priest ban the old pagan offerings.

Nearly Twelve Frostbitten Hours of Medieval Russian Folklore and Winter Magic

Set in the snow-buried wilderness of fourteenth-century northern Rus', Katherine Arden’s debut weaves luminous Historical Fantasy with authentic, shiver-inducing Folk Horror. In a remote wooden estate on the edge of the forest, young Vasilisa grows up listening to her nurse’s fireside tales of the *domovoi*, the house spirits, and Morozko, the blue-eyed frost demon—only Vasya can actually see them. When her widowed father brings home a devout Moscow stepmother and a charismatic, fanatical young priest who forbids the villagers from honoring the old household guardians, the protective wards begin to crumble and something ancient and hungry wakes beneath the snow.

Arden’s world feels startlingly tangible: the creak of frozen pines, the smell of hearth smoke and horses, and the suffocating dread of a medieval village turning against an unruly daughter who refuses to conform. Blending Folklore and dark Fairy Tale atmosphere with the stark witch-hunt tension of Slewfoot: A Tale of Bewitchery, it is an intoxicating entry point into one of modern fantasy’s finest trilogies.

At eleven hours and forty-eight minutes for a single Audible credit, *The Bear and the Nightingale* is a spellbinding winter listen that pulls you straight into The Girl in the Tower.

Narrator Soundcheck: Kathleen Gati

Hungarian-Canadian actor Kathleen Gati delivers an enchanting, richly textured performance across nearly twelve hours. Pronouncing every Russian name, patronymic, and folklore creature with natural, musical fluency, Gati shifts seamlessly from Vasya's fierce, youthful defiance to the chilling stillness of the Frost King and the feverish zealotry of Father Konstantin—turning the entire audiobook into a fireside Russian fairy tale.
