About this Audiobook

A sentient warship destroyed by treachery is reduced to a single human corpse-soldier, embarking on a cold, nineteen-year quest for revenge against the ruler of an empire.

The Historic Triple-Crown Space Opera of Sentient AI and Imperial Vengeance

The only novel to sweep the Hugo, Nebula, and Arthur C. Clarke Awards in a single year, *Ancillary Justice* is a groundbreaking twelve-hour space opera of revolutionary perspective, psychological subtlety, and quiet vengeance. Ann Leckie introduces the Radch Empire—an expansionist interstellar regime that conquers planets and replaces their populations with 'ancillaries,' captured human bodies neurologically linked to shipboard artificial intelligences. Protagonist Breq was once the *Justice of Toren*, a sentient warship who perceived reality simultaneously through thousands of human bodies, communications arrays, and engine telemetry. After an unspeakable betrayal destroys her ship, she is trapped in a solitary, fragile human form, driven by a burning purpose: revenge against the immortal, multi-bodied ruler of the empire.

Leckie’s brilliant worldbuilding challenges traditional science fiction tropes at every turn. In Radch culture, gender distinctions are nonexistent, with everyone referred to with female pronouns, forcing the listener to perceive characters purely through their actions, motivations, and moral choices rather than preconceived social expectations. In audio, Breq’s fragmented memory shifts seamlessly between her past glory as a godlike ship observing civilian subjugation and her present struggle as a lone traveler nursing an addicted officer back to health on an icy backwater world. The format captures the eerie, poetic dissonance of a consciousness mourning the tragic loss of her distributed self while confronting the cruel realities of imperial subjugation.

For listeners seeking an extraordinary return on a single credit, *Ancillary Justice* provides an unforgettable, intellectually profound listening experience. Adjoa Andoh delivers an astounding vocal performance, balancing Breq’s clipped, machine-like detachment with hidden reservoirs of grief and moral outrage. Beside kindred masterworks like A Memory Called Empire and The Player of Games, this historic twelve-hour listen is a must-have cornerstone of any serious Space Opera library, providing immense artistic payoff that stands the test of time and rewards close attention.

Narrator Soundcheck: Adjoa Andoh

Adjoa Andoh gives a tour-de-force narration that turns Leckie’s challenging narrative architecture into pure audio gold. Andoh captures Breq’s distinct perspective with astonishing nuance, using a precise, rhythmic cadence that suggests an artificial intelligence learning to navigate the clumsy emotional baggage of a singular biological body. Her vocal handling of Radch choral songs, aristocratic arrogance, and the chaotic gun battles on frozen frontier planets is utterly seamless, delivering an immersive, emotionally gripping twelve-hour performance that lingers long after the final chapter.
