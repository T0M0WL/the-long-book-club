About this Audiobook

Bonded by shared grief, two upstate New York widowers seek solace fishing the quiet creeks of the Catskills—until a diner cook tells them the dark local legend of Dutchman's Creek and the ancient sorcerer who still bargains with the dead.

Over Eleven Hypnotic Hours of Grief, Catskills Folklore, and Cosmic Dread

Winner of the Bram Stoker Award for Best Novel, John Langan’s *The Fisherman* is a quietly devastating triumph of literary Folk Horror and Cosmic Horror. Bound together by the unbearable loss of their wives, upstate New York widowers Abe and Dan find fragile solace fishing the quiet streams of the Catskills—until they stop at a roadside diner and hear the sprawling, century-old local legend of Dutchman’s Creek, a drowned valley, and a mysterious figure known as Der Fischer who promises to reel the dead back from an ancient black ocean.

Langan’s audacious Russian-doll structure—embedding a massive, gothic nineteenth-century chronicle directly inside Abe’s intimate modern-day narration—works like pure magic in the audio format. Because the entire novel is framed as a spoken tale passed from one bereaved man to another, listening feels like sitting across a dim tavern table while the rain lashes the windowpane, echoing the generational river-gothic sweep of Blackwater: The Complete Saga and the coastal unease of The Loney.

At eleven hours and twenty-nine minutes for a single Audible credit, *The Fisherman* is deeply moving, hauntingly strange, and utterly unforgettable.

Narrator Soundcheck: Danny Campbell

Danny Campbell's warm, weathered, unhurried voice is an uncanny match for Langan's fireside storytelling structure. Across eleven and a half hypnotic hours, Campbell embodies Abe's quiet grief with heartfelt restraint before leaning into the eerie, mythic cadence of the Dutchman's Creek chronicle—making the gradual descent from bereavement into Lovecraftian dread feel effortless and haunting.
