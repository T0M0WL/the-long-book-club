About this Audiobook

In 1965, three morally compromised mercenaries are hired to extract a young boy from a remote New Mexico backwoods cult, only to uncover a sprawling, parasitic evil beneath the forest floor that haunts them fifteen years later.

Nearly Sixteen Relentless Hours of Backwoods Cult Dread and Western Grit

Nick Cutter’s *Little Heaven* splices the sun-baked, hardboiled camaraderie of a classic western with the sickening, claustrophobic terror of backwoods Folk Horror. In 1965, three morally scarred guns-for-hire—Micah Shughrue, Minerva Atwater, and Ebenezer Elkins—are hired by a desperate woman to extract her nephew from a remote New Mexico forest compound led by a charismatic, unhinged preacher known as Father Amos. Fifteen years later, the survivors are dragged back into the same cursed woods when the ancient, parasitic entity sleeping beneath the settlement begins calling its debts due.

What makes *Little Heaven* such a memorable Horror audiobook is the way Cutter lets character bonds breathe before unleashing his trademark visceral Supernatural set pieces. Alternating cleanly between the 1965 extraction and the haunted 1980 reckoning, the narrative builds an oppressive sense of a landscape that is actively rotting from within—making it an ideal companion for listeners who devoured the woodland dread of The Ritual or the slow-burn rural menace of Harvest Home.

At fifteen hours and fifty-nine minutes for a single Audible credit, *Little Heaven* delivers epic, Stephen King-sized scope with a feral, uncompromising edge that rewards late-night listening.

Narrator Soundcheck: Corey Brill

Corey Brill brings a rugged, dusty, lived-in gravitas to nearly sixteen hours of New Mexico dread. His weathered baritone suits the hardbitten mercenary trio effortlessly, shifting smoothly from dry western banter to the unnerving, honeyed cadence of cult leader Father Amos—and ratcheting up the tension during Cutter's most stomach-churning forest encounters without ever slipping into melodrama.
