If We Were Villains

Author: M. L. Rio | Narrator: Robert Petkoff

Runtime: 12h 50m | Genre: Dark Academia, Psychological Thriller, Mystery, Literary Fiction

About this Audiobook

Ten years after a classmate's drowning at an elite conservatory, Oliver Marks finally tells the detective who put him away what really happened among his Shakespearean troupe.

A Intoxicating Thirteen-Hour Shakespearean Tragedy of Conservatory Rivalry and Murder

At the elite Dellecher Classical Conservatory, seven fourth-year theater students live, breathe, and perform Shakespeare until the lines between their onstage roles and private obsessions vanish completely. Oliver Marks has just finished serving a ten-year prison sentence for a murder he may or may not have committed, and on the day of his release, the retiring detective who arrested him demands the real truth. Looking back across a feverish autumn and winter of cast hierarchies, bruised egos, and whispered betrayals, Oliver recounts how a single casting decision fractured their tight-knit troupe into heroes, tyrants, and villains—culminating in a real-world tragedy beside a frozen lake.

In audio format, M. L. Rio's dialogue-driven screenplay structure finds its natural home. Rather than reading Shakespearean iambic pentameter silently off the page, hearing the troupe trade *Macbeth*, *Julius Caesar*, and *King Lear* speeches aloud restores the electric rhythm and theatrical vanity at the heart of the story. Positioned as a modern essential in our Dark Academia Collection alongside Donna Tartt's The Secret History, this nearly thirteen-hour production turns collegiate melodrama into an intimate, claustrophobic confession that pulls you straight into the rehearsal hall.

At twelve hours and fifty minutes, *If We Were Villains* offers crisp, propulsive pacing with zero filler for a single monthly credit. Rio captures the intoxicating intensity of artistic youth—when every jealousy feels fatal and every performance feels immortal—delivering a haunting emotional hangover that lingers long after the final scene. Spending a credit on this recording rewards listeners with one of the sharpest, most atmospheric campus mysteries in modern audio.

Narrator Soundcheck: Robert Petkoff

Robert Petkoff delivers a remarkably supple, theatrical performance across nearly thirteen hours, effortlessly shifting between quiet, prison-weary confession and full-throated Shakespearean declamation. He gives each member of the Dellecher troupe a distinct vocal posture—capturing Richard's booming arrogance, James's understated sensitivity, and Oliver's observant restraint without ever tipping into caricature. Clean studio mastering and natural dramatic pacing keep every rehearsal scene and police interrogation razor-sharp.
