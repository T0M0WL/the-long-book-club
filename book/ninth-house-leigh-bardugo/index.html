About this Audiobook

Haunted by a dark past, college freshman Alex Stern is recruited to monitor the blood-soaked occult rituals of Yale University's wealthiest secret societies.

Leigh Bardugo's Gritty Sixteen-Hour Yale Occult Mystery of Secret Societies and Blood Magic

Galaxy 'Alex' Stern is the most unlikely freshman ever admitted to Yale University. A high-school dropout raised in Los Angeles counterculture, Alex survived a horrific unsolved multiple homicide that left her with a terrifying gift: she can see 'Grays'—the restless ghosts drawn to New Haven's ancient, magic-soaked campus. Handpicked by the shadowy Lethe House to act as watchdog over Yale's eight wealthiest secret societies, Alex must monitor grotesque rituals, prognostication using human entrails, and necromantic bargains with the dead. When a local girl is brutally murdered on campus while Alex's mentor Darlington is mysteriously missing, her surveillance duty turns into a desperate fight for survival.

On audio, Leigh Bardugo's dark urban fantasy pulses with brooding New England atmosphere and sharp social bite. Hearing the stark contrast between Alex's street-hardened cynicism and the pompous, centuries-old traditions of Tomb & Key or Skull & Bones makes every secret society basement feel palpably claustrophobic. Highlighted in our Dark Academia Collection alongside Babel and Katabasis, this sixteen-hour production blends gritty noir detective work with visceral occult horror in a way that feels built for late-night headphone listening.

Clocking in at over sixteen hours for a single Audible credit, *Ninth House* delivers rich world-building, fierce emotional stakes, and relentless mystery momentum. Bardugo refuses to sanitize the cost of privilege or magic, crafting an unforgettable heroine who refuses to break even when surrounded by monsters in tweed coats. Investing a monthly token here launches one of contemporary fantasy's most addictive, atmospheric campus sagas.

Narrator Soundcheck: Lauren Fortgang, Michael David Axtell

Lauren Fortgang and Michael David Axtell form an ideal dual-narration pairing for Bardugo's dual-timeline structure. Fortgang anchors Alex Stern's chapters with bruised, gravelly resilience and biting deadpan wit, making her survival instincts immediately believable. Axtell counters brilliantly in Darlington's flashbacks, adopting a polished, earnest academic cadence that underscores his tragic idealism. Crisp audio production and seamless perspective shifts keep all sixteen hours gripping from start to finish.
