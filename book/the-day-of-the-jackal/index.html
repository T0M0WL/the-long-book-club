About this Audiobook

An anonymous English assassin contracted by French extremists stalks Charles de Gaulle with a custom rifle, pursued by an exhausted Parisian detective.

The Flawless Mechanized Procedural That Revolutionized the Modern Manhunt

Clocking in at thirteen relentless hours, *The Day of the Jackal* is Frederick Forsyth’s astonishing debut novel, a clockwork thriller of such mathematical precision and documentary realism that it forever redefined the procedural espionage genre. Set in 1963 against the volatile backdrop of the Algerian War, the plot ignites when the extremist OAS military syndicate realizes its ranks are thoroughly compromised by French intelligence. In desperation, they look outside France, contracting an aloof, blond English professional assassin known only by his code name: the Jackal. His fee is half a million dollars, and his target is the most heavily guarded statesman on earth, General Charles de Gaulle.

What makes Forsyth’s novel an auditory tour de force is its meticulous, dual-track narrative structure. On one side, we follow the Jackal as he prepares his hit with cold, sociopathic genius: acquiring stolen British birth certificates, practicing long-range marksmanship in the Belgian countryside, and commissioning an ingenious customized sniper rifle that can be disassembled and disguised as an aluminum crutch. On the other side is Commissaire Claude Lebel, an unassuming French investigator granted emergency powers across Whitehall, Interpol, and the Sûreté to identify a phantom whose face no one has seen. Listening in audio heightens the breathless suspense as the two men move across Europe toward a catastrophic convergence on Liberation Day in Paris.

For listeners looking for an airtight, utterly unputdownable thriller that maximizes a single monthly credit, *The Day of the Jackal* is a peerless masterpiece of suspense tradecraft. David Rintoul delivers an authoritative, ice-cold performance that complements Forsyth’s journalism-rooted procedural detail with terrifying poise and relentless momentum. Paired with companion manhunts like Eye of the Needle and I Am Pilgrim, it remains the gold standard of high-stakes international Spy Thriller storytelling, offering pulse-pounding value that stands completely unchallenged after half a century.

Narrator Soundcheck: David Rintoul

David Rintoul gives a chillingly precise, razor-sharp narration that suits Forsyth’s clinical prose to perfection. Rintoul’s commanding Scottish-tinged baritone carries an air of cold calculation, lending the Jackal an unnerving, polished charm that makes his sociopathic efficiency deeply hypnotic to hear aloud. Rintoul handles rapid dialogue transitions between French prefects, English passport forgers, and Belgian gunsmiths with effortless linguistic flair, sustaining relentless momentum and unbearable tension across thirteen electrifying hours.
