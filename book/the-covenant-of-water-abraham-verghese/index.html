An Epic Multigenerational Saga of Faith, Medicine, and Family Secrets in Kerala

Abraham Verghese crafts a breathtaking, multi-generational masterpiece set amidst the lush backwaters of Kerala on South India's Malabar Coast. Spanning nearly eight decades from 1900 to 1977, the story begins with a twelve-year-old girl sent by boat to an arranged marriage with a forty-year-old widower, eventually growing into the revered matriarch known as Big Ammachi. Across three generations of births, tragedies, and medical triumphs, the family grapples with a mysterious affliction known simply as "the Condition"—a hereditary quirk wherein at least one member in every generation dies by drowning. Verghese blends surgical precision with profound human empathy to create a timeless portrait of love, faith, community, generational resilience, and scientific healing across decades of change.

Hearing the author himself narrate this sweeping family chronicle elevates the listening experience into something deeply sacred, intimate, and transformative. Verghese’s lyrical, musical voice breathes authentic warmth into the Malayalam dialogue, the murmuring rhythm of the monsoon rain, and the rich culinary traditions of southwest India.
