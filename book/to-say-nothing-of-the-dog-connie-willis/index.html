About this Audiobook

Suffering from severe time-lag after repeated jumps to 1940 Coventry, 21st-century Oxford historian Ned Henry is sent to 1888 Victorian England for rest—only to spark romantic chaos over a hideous bishop's bird stump.

Twenty-One Hours of Hugo-Winning Victorian Screwball Comedy and Time-Travel Farce

After weeks of gruelling temporal jumps back to bombed-out 1940 Coventry Cathedral hunting an eccentric benefactor's hideous ornamental 'bishop's bird stump,' twenty-first-century Oxford historian Ned Henry is suffering acute time-lag—hallucinating bells, losing nouns, and falling asleep standing up. Desperate to hide him from tyrannical donor Lady Schrapnell, Professor Dunworthy dispatches Ned to the soothing calm of 1888 Victorian England for two weeks of rest. Naturally, rest proves impossible once Ned winds up rowing down the Thames with feuding aristocrats, missing cats, spiritualist seances, and fellow time-traveler Verity Kindle.

Listening to *To Say Nothing of the Dog* on audio reveals why Connie Willis's Hugo Award winner stands as one of the sharpest tonal pivots in Science Fiction. Paying affectionate homage to Jerome K. Jerome's *Three Men in a Boat* alongside P.G. Wodehouse and Agatha Christie, this joyous mashup of Time Travel, Comedy, and sunlit Victorian Historical Fiction balances featherlight romantic Humour with genuine timeline peril.

Clocking in at twenty hours and fifty-eight minutes for a single Audible credit, *To Say Nothing of the Dog* is pure restorative joy—proving time-travel epics can make you laugh out loud just as fiercely as Doomsday Book made you weep.
