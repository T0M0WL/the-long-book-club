About this Audiobook

Gravely wounded during the final months of the American Civil War, Confederate soldier W.P. Inman deserts a military hospital and sets out on a perilous journey on foot across the Blue Ridge Mountains to return to Ada, the woman waiting for him at Cold Mountain.

Over Fourteen Haunting Hours Walking Home Through the Ravaged Blue Ridge Mountains

Winner of the National Book Award for Fiction, Charles Frazier’s *Cold Mountain* recasts Homer’s *Odyssey* across the blood-soaked, collapsing Confederacy of 1864. Badly wounded at the Battle of the Crater, disillusioned soldier W.P. Inman slips out of a coastal hospital and begins an arduous, dangerous trek on foot across North Carolina toward the Blue Ridge farm of Ada Monroe—a sheltered Charleston minister's daughter who, aided by the fiercely resourceful drifter Ruby Thewes, is learning how to survive a starving home front.

What makes *Cold Mountain* such an extraordinary American Civil War listen is its tactile, unhurried poetry. Rather than dwelling on grand strategy, Frazier immerses you in the granular reality of nineteenth-century Americana—the names of wild herbs, the menace of Home Guard bounty hunters, winter hunger, and the quiet resilience of two battered souls trying to remember who they were before the War. Pairing beautifully with the sweeping Southern panorama of Gone with the Wind and the battlefield intensity of The Killer Angels, it is Historical Fiction of the highest literary caliber.

At fourteen hours and twenty-six minutes for a single Audible credit, *Cold Mountain* is an aching, deeply atmospheric journey that stays lodged in your memory long after the final chapter.

Narrator Soundcheck: Charles Frazier

Narrated by Charles Frazier himself, this fourteen-and-a-half-hour recording is one of those rare author performances that genuinely elevates the text. Frazier's unhurried, gently weathered North Carolina drawl fits his lyrical Appalachian prose like a hand in a worn leather glove—giving Inman's weary resolve, Ada's quiet dignity, and Ruby's sharp mountain pragmatism an unmistakable authenticity no studio actor could fake.
