About this Audiobook

In the sprawling, soot-choked metropolis of New Crobuzon—where alchemical steam engines, cactus-people, and Garuda bird-men collide—renegade scientist Isaac Dan der Grimnebulin accidentally unleashes a nightmare that feeds on conscious thought.

China Miéville's Twenty-Six-Hour Steampunk and New Weird Masterpiece of Alchemical Horror

Welcome to New Crobuzon: a sprawling, soot-stained industrial metropolis straddling the ribs of an ancient leviathan, where Victorian smokestacks, Babbage-style computational engines, and union strikes collide with bio-thaumaturgical body modification. When eccentric human scientist Isaac Dan der Grimnebulin accepts a commission from Yagharek—an exiled Garuda bird-man stripped of his wings—to restore the power of flight, his experiments with an illegal grub accidentally unleash the Slake-Moths: multi-dimensional predators that hypnoticize citizens and devour their conscious minds.

Listening to *Perdido Street Station* on audio is an unforgettable sensory experience. Because China Miéville's prose is famously lush, baroque, and packed with Dickensian street slang, having a master narrator guide you through New Crobuzon's districts turns overwhelming print density into operatic world-building. Serving as the Epic Audio Marathon on our Steampunk hub alongside Senlin Ascends and The Difference Engine, this nearly twenty-six-hour titan is where Steampunk and Cosmic Horror fuse.

Spanning twenty-five hours and fifty-two minutes for a single Audible credit, *Perdido Street Station* offers immense world-building value for listeners who want their Fantasy grimy, politically sharp, and wildly original.

Legendary British narrator John Lee—already celebrated on our shelves for *The Count of Monte Cristo* and *The Pillars of the Earth*—is a natural match for Miéville's grand, theatrical prose. Lee navigates Khepri scarab-headed artists, Vodyanoi water-sculptors, and corrupt parliamentarians with authoritative baritone resonance and crisp diction across nearly twenty-six hours.
