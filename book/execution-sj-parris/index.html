Sixteen and a Half Hours Undercover Inside the Infamous 1586 Babington Plot

Summoned back across the Channel in 1586 with vital French intelligence, Giordano Bruno expects gratitude from Sir Francis Walsingham—only to be thrust into England's deadliest undercover sting. Idealistic young Catholic gentlemen led by Anthony Babington are plotting to assassinate Queen Elizabeth and rescue Mary, Queen of Scots from her Staffordshire prison. Rather than arrest them immediately, Walsingham is deliberately letting the conspiracy fester until Mary condemns herself in writing—and orders Bruno to join the plotters from the inside while deciphering their secret codes.

In audio, *Execution* stands as one of S.J. Parris's tautest feats of Political Intrigue and Historical Mystery. Even when listeners know how history judged Mary Stuart, Parris turns double-agent loyalties and cipher-breaking into agonizing Whodunnit and Historical Fiction suspense.

Spanning sixteen hours and thirty-three minutes for a single Audible credit, *Execution* captures the moral chill of Walsingham's surveillance state at the height of Tudor power.

Daniel Philpott settles completely into the role across all sixteen and a half hours of *Execution*. His nuanced performance conveys Bruno's mounting disgust with torture chambers and double-crosses, contrasting sharply against Anthony Babington's reckless vanity and Walsingham's icy pragmatism.
