Nineteen Hours of Heatwave Murder and Stolen Alchemy in Thomas Cromwell's Final Summer

In the blistering summer of 1540, London is choked with drought, political terror, and plague rumors—and Thomas Cromwell's hold on Henry VIII's favour is slipping by the hour. Lawyer Matthew Shardlake has sworn to leave Whitehall's murderous intrigues behind after Dissolution, devoting himself to his quiet Chancery practice. Instead, Cromwell blackmails him with twelve frantic days to clear a mute teenager accused of boiling a servant alive—and simultaneously recover the lost, devastating formula for ancient Greek Fire.

On audio, *Dark Fire* exchanges the snowbound cloisters of Book 1 for foul, sun-baked Tudor alleys, introducing the roguish assistant Jack Barak as an unforgettable foil to Shardlake's conscience. Blending forensic Whodunnit suspense with cutthroat Political Intrigue, Sansom drops listeners straight into the ticking clock of Historical Mystery and gritty Historical Fiction.

At eighteen hours and fifty-three minutes for a single credit, *Dark Fire* is taut, sweaty, and impossible to pause—one of the very best Tudor legal thrillers ever recorded.

Narrator Soundcheck: Steven Crossley

Returning after his acclaim in Book 1, Steven Crossley elevates every page of *Dark Fire* across nearly nineteen hours. Crossley gives rough-edged newcomer Jack Barak immediate Cockney swagger, nails Cromwell's razor-sharp menace, and makes the frantic courtroom clock genuinely breathless.
