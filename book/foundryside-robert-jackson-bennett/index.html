About this Audiobook

In the merchant-ruled city of Tevanne, scriving engraves commands on inanimate objects to rewrite reality's laws. When a talented thief steals a sentient key, she ignites an industrial war.

Robert Jackson Bennett’s Reality-Coding Cyberpunk Fantasy Heist Masterpiece in Audio

In the sprawling merchant city of Tevanne, magic is an industrial enterprise known as scriving—the art of engraving ancient sigils onto inanimate objects to convince them that the laws of physics do not apply. By tricking a wheel into believing it is constantly rolling downhill or convincing stone that it is weightless, four ruthless merchant houses have monopolized global commerce and subjugated millions into slums. Sancia Grado is a brilliant gutter thief with a magical metal plate in her skull that allows her to feel the presence of scrived objects. When she is hired to steal an ancient artifact from a heavily fortified warehouse, she inadvertently liberates Clef: an ancient, sentient key capable of unlocking any scrived ward and rewriting reality itself.

In audio, narrator Tara Sands transforms Bennett’s nineteen-hour techno-magical heist into pure adrenaline. Sands imbues Sancia with streetwise snark, vulnerability, and fierce protective instincts, while voicing Clef with delightfully pompous, ancient eccentricity. Featured as a standout cornerstone of our Hard Magic Systems collection alongside The Lies of Locke Lamora and Mistborn: The Final Empire, this recording turns the analytical rules of computer code and magical physics into high-octane entertainment.

Narrator Soundcheck: Tara Sands

Tara Sands delivers a vibrant, razor-sharp performance across nineteen and a half hours, perfectly capturing the gritty energy of Tevanne's underworld. Sands gives Sancia an authentic, cynical street edge that never loses its core vulnerability, and her comedic timing during Sancia’s telepathic banter with the sentient key Clef is impeccable. Supporting characters like the principled ex-soldier Gregor Dandolo and the brilliant scholar Orso are instantly recognizable, supported by crisp, modern audio engineering.
