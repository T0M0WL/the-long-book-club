About this Audiobook

When editor Susan Ryeland discovers the final chapter of a bestselling crime manuscript is missing—and its author dead—she must solve two intertwined murders. A genius 15-hour meta-mystery dual-narrated by Samantha Bond and Allan Corduner.

Anthony Horowitz’s 15-Hour Dual-Narrated Meta-Mystery Masterpiece

An ingenious Russian doll of a mystery, *Magpie Murders* contains an authentic 1950s golden-age detective novel tucked neatly inside a modern-day publishing murder. Literary editor Susan Ryeland is handed the handwritten manuscript of bestselling author Alan Conway’s latest whodunnit, featuring German sleuth Atticus Pünd investigating a suspicious death in a sleepy English village. But as Susan reaches the climax, she realizes the manuscript's final chapter is missing—and Conway has plunged from his country manor's tower to his death.

What could have been an overwhelming typographical trick on the page turns into pure listening theater thanks to brilliant dual casting. Allan Corduner voices the Conway manuscript with all the velvety, mid-century BBC polish of a vintage radio drama, while Samantha Bond steps in as Susan Ryeland with razor-sharp modern wit. As featured in our Best Long Cozy Crime Audiobooks hub and alongside cerebral brain-teasers like The First Fifteen Lives of Harry August, Horowitz delivers two full-length novels for the price of one.

Clocking in at fifteen hours and fifteen minutes, *Magpie Murders* delivers supreme runtime economy for a single Audible credit. You are treated to two meticulously crafted, fully resolved murder mysteries packed with anagrams, red herrings, and brilliant clues hidden in plain sight. For fans of Agatha Christie and Golden Age detective craft who demand maximum replay value, this dual-voiced labyrinth is essential listening.

Narrator Soundcheck: Allan Corduner, Samantha Bond

The decision to divide narrative duties between Samantha Bond and Allan Corduner is a stroke of audio genius. Corduner captures Atticus Pünd’s gentle, melancholy cadence and the arch eccentricities of a 1950s English village with sublime elegance, pacing the period mystery with theatrical poise. When the manuscript breaks and Samantha Bond assumes control as the grounded, dryly observant Susan Ryeland, the tonal shift is electric. Both performers demonstrate flawless articulation, handling an enormous cast of suspects without a single stumble in consistency across 15 intricate hours.
