Simon Sebag Montefiore's 27-Hour Epic of Imperial Power, Extravagance, and Ruin

Spanning over twenty-seven gripping hours, Simon Sebag Montefiore’s *The Romanovs: 1613–1918* is a monumental, blood-soaked intimate chronicle of the extraordinary dynasty that conquered one-sixth of the earth’s surface. From the election of a terrified sixteen-year-old boy in a besieged monastery in 1613 to the brutal basement executions of Nicholas II and his family at Ekaterinburg in 1918, Montefiore strips away romanticized myths to expose the sheer, intoxicating reality of autocratic power. Twenty tsars and tsarinas stride across this vast temporal canvas—some brilliant, visionary empire-builders like Peter the Great and Catherine the Great, others paranoid conspirators, syphilitic tyrants, and religious zealots—each governing a sprawling empire where imperial succession was perpetually decided by poison, midnight bayonets, and ruthless court factionalism.

In audio format, the spoken word elevates Montefiore’s meticulous archival research into a mesmerizing, immersive imperial chamber opera. Rather than getting bogged down in dry dates and diplomatic treaties, the performance brings out the eccentric psychological quirks, scandalous love letters, and lethal paranoia that drove the Russian court across three centuries. Listening aloud transforms the narrative from an academic history into an intimate, high-stakes drama of survival, statecraft, and human frailty.
