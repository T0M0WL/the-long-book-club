About this Audiobook

Seeking a quieter life, Manhattan artist Ned Constantine moves his family to the idyllic New England village of Cornwall Coombe—where the townsfolk still practice ancient agricultural rituals governed by the mysterious Widow Fortune.

Thomas Tryon's Fifteen-Hour 1973 Blueprint for American Folk Horror

Published in the same landmark year as *The Wicker Man* (1973), Thomas Tryon's *Harvest Home* is the foundational cornerstone of American folk horror—explicitly cited by Stephen King as the direct inspiration for *Children of the Corn*. Seeking an escape from Manhattan's smog and his daughter's chronic asthma, commercial artist Ned Constantine relocates his family to the picturesque, time-capsule village of Cornwall Coombe in Connecticut. The villagers shun modern machinery, grow impossibly lush golden corn, and obey the gentle, herbalist matriarch known as the Widow Fortune. Yet as the autumn festival of Harvest Home approaches, Ned's curiosity about a disgraced former villager uncovers the terrifying price of the town's bounty.

Listening to *Harvest Home* on audio magnifies its slow, sun-dappled sense of claustrophobia. Because the horror builds through folksy New England hospitality, seasonal planting songs, and whispered taboos before tightening like a noose in the October cornfields, a skilled narrator makes you feel Ned's isolation in your bones. Serving as the Origin Foundation on our Folk Horror hub alongside Slewfoot: A Tale of Bewitchery, this fifteen-hour classic is essential listening for horror historians.

Clocking in at fifteen hours and eighteen minutes for one monthly credit, *Harvest Home* offers rich autumn atmosphere, masterclass slow-burn tension, and one of the most haunting finales in twentieth-century horror.
