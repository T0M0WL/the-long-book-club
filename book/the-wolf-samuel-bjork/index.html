Thirteen and a Half Hours Tracing Holger Munch and Mia Krüger's Very First Case

Eight years before the shocking events of I'm Travelling Alone, veteran investigator Holger Munch is newly promoted to lead Oslo's special homicide unit—and facing a nightmare. On a misty field outside the city, the bodies of two eleven-year-old boys are discovered meticulously staged beside a slain white hare, echoing an unsolved double murder across the Swedish border years earlier. Desperate for fresh eyes, Munch summons an extraordinary twenty-two-year-old police academy cadet named Mia Krüger—already haunted by her own family shadows.

In audio format, *The Wolf* (published in parts of Europe as *White as Snow*) works equally well as an origin story for longtime fans and a chilling entry point into Nordic Noir and methodical Police Procedural storytelling alongside our Nordic Noir & Scandi Crime favorites. Blending bleak Nordic Crime realism with deep Psychological Thriller trauma, it illuminates why Munch trusts Mia's instincts above all else.

Spanning thirteen hours and twenty-five minutes for one credit, *The Wolf* delivers Samuel Bjørk's longest, richest psychological hunt yet.

Narrator Soundcheck: Laura Paton

In her longest series outing at thirteen and a half hours, Laura Paton gives a remarkably tender performance voicing a younger, rawer Mia Krüger just stepping out of the police academy. Paton balances the bleak Swedish-Norwegian case history with genuine empathy as Munch and Mia's legendary partnership forms.
