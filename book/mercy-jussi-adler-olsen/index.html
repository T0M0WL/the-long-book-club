About this Audiobook

Banished to the basement of Copenhagen police headquarters, cynical detective Carl Mørck and his Syrian assistant Assad reopen the five-year-old disappearance of a rising politician presumed drowned at sea, discovering she is still alive in a lightless pressure chamber.

Pure Claustrophobic Terror Balanced by Copenhagen’s Greatest Unlikely Detective Pairing

Few thrillers manage to marry unbearable physical claustrophobia with dry workplace cynicism as effortlessly as Jussi Adler-Olsen’s *Mercy* (published internationally as *The Keeper of Lost Causes*). Shunted into the neglected basement of Copenhagen police headquarters to head up a newly funded cold-case division, burnout detective Carl Mørck plans to spend his remaining working years smoking, drinking lukewarm coffee, and doing as little as humanly possible. That comfortable inertia shatters when his bizarrely capable Syrian assistant, Assad, refuses to let dormant murder files rest, pushing Mørck to reopen the vanished case of Merete Lynggaard—a charismatic rising politician who mysteriously vanished from a passenger ferry five years earlier.

What elevates *Mercy* into the top tier of international audio procedurals is its harrowing dual-perspective structure, brought to life with staggering virtuosity by narrator Steven Pacey. While Mørck and Assad slowly piece together yellowing clues and interview forgotten suspects across Denmark, the narrative cuts relentlessly to Merete’s ongoing ordeal inside a windowless, lightless, pressurized concrete chamber. Pacey—celebrated worldwide for his legendary vocal work on Joe Abercrombie’s The Blade Itself—infuses Carl with world-weary Danish sarcasm while turning Merete’s desperate battle against sensory deprivation into audio theatre so tense it feels genuinely suffocating. If you relish the dry procedural dark humour of The Bat, Department Q’s basement dynamic is pure gold dust.

At just over 13 hours of tightly calibrated suspense, *Mercy* represents peak credit value for mystery and procedural purists who demand narrative economy over needless filler. Adler-Olsen crafts an airtight, terrifying race against the clock where every clue clicks into place with satisfying mechanical precision, while establishing an unforgettable detective duo whose chemistry only deepens across subsequent cases. As the subterranean temperature drops and the air pressure mounts, you will find yourself checking your own breathing alongside Merete. It is a triumphant masterclass in Danish suspense that proves a single Audible credit can deliver more white-knuckle adrenaline than a dozen Hollywood thrillers.

Narrator Soundcheck: Steven Pacey

British narration titan Steven Pacey delivers a masterclass in the UK edition, imbuing Carl Mørck with an aching, cynical Danish cadence that makes his sarcasm crackle with dry wit, while lending Assad a disarming warmth masking lethal competence. Pacey’s harrowing portrayal of Merete Lynggaard’s descent into sensory deprivation—his rasping whispers, shallow panicked breaths, and unyielding psychological stamina—turns her captivity into an unforgettable, nerve-shredding audio experience. (North American listeners will encounter Erik Davies’s solid, gritty performance in The Keeper of Lost Causes, though Pacey’s definitive reading remains the crown jewel).
