The Trouble with Peace

Author: Joe Abercrombie | Narrator: Steven Pacey

Runtime: 22h 55m | Genre: Grimdark Fantasy, Epic Fantasy, Dark Fantasy, Industrial Fantasy, Military Fantasy, Court Intrigue

About this Audiobook

Conspiracy and open treason threaten the Union throne as King Orso faces bitter rebellion from ambitious nobles and simmering northern unrest.

Treason, Pride, and the Tragic Inevitability of Civil War in Adua

Spanning nearly twenty-three electrifying, razor-sharp hours, *The Trouble with Peace* is the brilliant middle chapter of Joe Abercrombie’s *Age of Madness* trilogy, outstripping its predecessor with unmatched political suspense and military spectacle. Following the bloody skirmishes of A Little Hatred, fragile peace settles across the Union, but peace is the most dangerous condition of all. In Adua, King Orso ascends a throne crippled by immense foreign debt, domestic labor riots, and entrenched bureaucratic rot. Despite being dismissed as an indolent fop, Orso displays genuine empathy and sharp political instinct, yet finds himself surrounded by treacherous ministers and ambitious provincial lords hungry for power.

Simultaneously, Leo dan Brock’s insatiable vanity and frustration with the royal court make him easy prey for conspirators whispering of grand rebellions. Pushed forward by his ambitious wife Savine—who is fighting tooth and nail to rebuild her financial empire while grappling with severe trauma—Leo forms a treasonous alliance that marches the Union toward catastrophic civil war. Meanwhile, in the frost-bitten North, Rikke utilizes her perilous visions to defend the Protectorate against the vicious warlord Stour Nightfall. Abercrombie’s depiction of military logistics, the horrific realities of pitched combat, and the agonizing ironies of conflicting loyalties is nothing short of breathtaking.

Steven Pacey delivers another flawless vocal exhibition, capturing King Orso’s wounded self-deprecation, Savine’s calculating poise, and Broad’s simmering, reluctant brutality with effortless nuance. Every confrontation crackles with acoustic energy, making the hours fly past with relentless momentum. It is a brilliant companion to grimdark masterworks like Best Served Cold and The Heroes. For listeners who value supreme character psychology, grand military maneuvers, and peerless voice acting, this audiobook is an absolute triumph.

Narrator Soundcheck: Steven Pacey

Steven Pacey’s narration on The Trouble with Peace is a clinic in emotional subtlety and battlefield grandeur. Pacey gives King Orso an irresistible, self-effacing warmth that makes his political peril deeply moving, while contrasting it sharply with Leo dan Brock’s petulant bravado. During the climactic tactical battles, his pacing accelerates with visceral urgency, delivering Abercrombie’s brutal prose with chilling authenticity. Produced with flawless acoustic fidelity, this twenty-three-hour production is an audio marvel that demands to be heard.
