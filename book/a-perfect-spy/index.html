About this Audiobook

A celebrated British diplomat vanishes into a seaside boarding house to write a secret confession explaining why a lifetime of filial manipulation made him a traitor.

John le Carré's Deeply Autobiographical Masterpiece of Lifelong Deception

Clocking in at an immense twenty hours on its unabridged UK release, *A Perfect Spy* is John le Carré’s most intimate, psychologically complex, and deeply autobiographical triumph—hailed by Philip Roth as the best English novel of the postwar era. The story ignites when Magnus Pym, an accomplished senior British diplomat and high-ranking secret intelligence officer in Vienna, abruptly vanishes following his father's funeral. While frantic manhunts are mounted across Europe by MI6, the CIA, and Czech counterintelligence, Pym has sequestered himself in a boarding house on the Devon coast, frantically typing an unvarnished confession to his son to explain how a charismatic fraud, con-man, and loving father groomed him into becoming the most devastating double agent in British history.

What makes this novel an auditory tour de force is its unflinching excavation of the psychological roots of espionage. Magnus Pym is not a traitor driven by ideology or greed, but by an innate compulsion to please every person he encounters, a trait instilled by his flamboyant, sociopathic con-artist father, Rick Pym (modeled directly on le Carré’s own father, Ronnie Cornwell). In audio, the shifting timelines—from Pym’s chaotic childhood in shady boarding schools and postwar Switzerland to diplomatic safe houses in Washington and Berlin—build a devastating portrait of identity fracture. Every secret meeting with his lifelong Czech controller Axel pulses with emotional ambiguity, blurring the boundary between genuine human friendship and ruthless professional manipulation.
