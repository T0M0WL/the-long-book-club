About this Audiobook

Umberto Eco's Legendary Twenty-One-Hour Medieval Abbey Labyrinth and Murder Mystery

In the winter of 1327, Franciscan friar William of Baskerville—a former inquisitor gifted with razor-sharp deductive logic—arrives alongside his young Benedictine novice Adso of Melk at a wealthy, isolated abbey in northern Italy. Summoned to mediate a perilous theological dispute between the Franciscan order and papal envoys, William instead finds the monastery gripped by terror: monks are dying in bizarre, apocalyptic circumstances tied to a forbidden octagonal library known as the Aedificium. Armed with empiricism and a magnifying lens, William must decode secret ciphers and outwit fanatical inquisitors before the seventh trumpet sounds.

On audio, Umberto Eco's towering literary masterpiece sheds the intimidating reputation it sometimes carries in print. Rather than stumbling over unfamiliar medieval heresies or labyrinthine architectural descriptions, listeners are swept effortlessly forward by the Holmes-and-Watson camaraderie between William and Adso inside snowbound stone cloisters. Anchoring our Dark Academia Collection alongside The Historian and The Shadow of the Wind, this twenty-one-hour classic celebrates the dangerous, intoxicating power of forbidden books.

Spanning twenty-one hours and five minutes for a single monthly credit, *The Name of the Rose* is an extraordinary intellectual and atmospheric investment. Eco weaves historical thriller, philosophical detective story, and gothic monastic dread into a timeless monument of world literature that rewards every hour spent inside its shadowed scriptorium.

Narrator Soundcheck: Sean Barrett

Veteran narrator Sean Barrett delivers an authoritative, deeply atmospheric masterclass across twenty-one hours. Barrett voices William of Baskerville with wry, Sherlockian intelligence and calm inquisitiveness, while giving the elderly narrator Adso a warm, reflective cadence looking back on his youth. His handling of Latin phrases, medieval scholastic debates, and sinister inquisitorial confrontations is effortless and crisp, backed by clean studio acoustics that keep every clue resonant.
