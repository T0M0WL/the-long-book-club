About this Audiobook

Eleven-year-old poison enthusiast Flavia de Luce must clear her father's name when a dying stranger is dumped in their cucumber patch. A wickedly funny 6.5-hour English country house mystery narrated by Jayne Entwistle.

Alan Bradley’s English Country Mystery and the Birth of Flavia de Luce

Set in rural England in the summer of 1950, *The Sweetness at the Bottom of the Pie* introduces one of the most delightful, macabre young heroines in modern detective fiction. Flavia de Luce is an eleven-year-old prodigy with an obsessive passion for poison chemistry, operating out of a Victorian laboratory in her eccentric family’s crumbling mansion, Buckshaw. When a dead snipe with a postage stamp pinned to its beak is left on their doorstep, followed hours later by the discovery of a dying man in the estate’s cucumber patch, Flavia is utterly thrilled—until her aloof father is arrested for the crime.

Alan Bradley’s debut novel is a triumph of voice, and in narrator Jayne Entwistle, Flavia found her match made in auditory heaven. Entwistle channels Flavia’s haughty aristocratic dignity, brilliant scientific deductions, and sibling skirmishes with sparkling comedic timing. As showcased in our Best Long Cozy Crime Audiobooks collection and alongside classic English manor puzzles like Magpie Murders, Entwistle turns what could be an annoying child prodigy into an utterly lovable, laugh-out-loud heroine.

Running over six and a half hours of vintage English atmosphere, chemical lore, and philatelic intrigue, *The Sweetness at the Bottom of the Pie* delivers delightful listening value. Bradley’s prose is dense with historical charm, witty dialogue, and genuine mystery mechanics that keep you guessing until the final revelation. For listeners looking for a fresh, darkly funny twist on the traditional English village murder, this is an irresistible one-credit discovery.

Narrator Soundcheck: Jayne Entwistle

Jayne Entwistle’s narration of Flavia de Luce is pure audio perfection. She avoids the high-pitched cliches often assigned to child characters, instead voicing Flavia with an imperious, clipped mid-Atlantic delivery that conveys both her staggering intellectual brilliance and her secret childhood vulnerabilities. Her vocal versatility is equally impressive when portraying Flavia’s exasperated, postage-stamp-obsessed father, her spiteful elder sisters Ophelia and Daphne, and the bewildered local village police inspector. The recording is pristinely produced, brimming with wit and charm.
