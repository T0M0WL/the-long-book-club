About this Audiobook

After a catastrophic heatwave in India, a newly established UN agency in Zurich fights alongside scientists, central bankers, and eco-radicals to re-engineer the global economy and save Earth's biosphere.

Kim Stanley Robinson's Monumental Twenty-Hour Polyphonic Blueprint for Saving the Planet

Opening with a devastating, unforgettable heatwave in Uttar Pradesh that claims twenty million lives in a single week, *The Ministry for the Future* refuses to look away from the climate crisis—and refuses to surrender to nihilism. Set in the near future, the novel tracks Mary Murphy, head of a newly established United Nations agency in Zurich tasked with advocating for future generations, alongside surviving aid worker Frank May, glaciologists in Antarctica, central bankers, and covert eco-saboteurs. Together, they wage a multi-decade battle to re-engineer global finance, geo-stabilize melting glaciers, and build a survivable, post-carbon civilization.

Listening to *The Ministry for the Future* on audio is where Robinson's ambitious mosaic structure truly comes alive. Because the book alternates between tight character drama, eyewitness oral histories from around the globe, and riddles told by photons and carbon atoms, the multi-narrator ensemble turns what could read like a policy dossier in print into a gripping, documentary-style global thriller reminiscent of *World War Z*. Anchoring our Solarpunk hub, this nearly twenty-one-hour production bridges the gap between Post-Apocalyptic Survival and pragmatic hope.

At twenty hours and forty-two minutes for a single Audible credit, *The Ministry for the Future* is one of the most urgent, intellectually exhilarating science fiction listens of the twenty-first century. It rewards your token with big-canvas ideas, hard scientific plausibility, and the rare thrill of watching humanity actually fight back.
