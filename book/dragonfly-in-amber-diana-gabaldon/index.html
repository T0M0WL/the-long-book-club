About this Audiobook

In 1744 Paris, Claire and Jamie Fraser infiltrate the decadent court of King Louis XV in a desperate bid to avert the catastrophic Jacobite rebellion at Culloden.

Versailles Intrigue, the Jacobite Tragedy, and the Broken Heart of the Scottish Highlands

Spanning forty-three and a half breathtaking hours, *Dragonfly in Amber* is Diana Gabaldon’s monumental second installment in the *Outlander* saga, deepening the historical grandeur of Outlander. Opening in 1968 Scotland, a grieving Claire Randall brings her grown daughter Brianna to the Highlands, preparing to reveal the astonishing truth of her parentage. The narrative then plunges back into 1744 Paris, where Claire and Jamie Fraser navigate the decadent, poison-laced court of King Louis XV in an audacious espionage mission to prevent the slaughter at Culloden Moor.

Gabaldon’s historical scholarship shines with dazzling brilliance, contrasting the gilded salons and occult societies of pre-revolutionary France against the brutal reality of Highland warfare. As Claire and Jamie attempt to bankrupt Prince Charles Edward Stuart and sabotage French financing, their private lives are battered by physical trauma, pregnancy loss, and the looming inevitability of historical fate. When their schemes collapse and history marches toward the bloodbath of April 1746, Gabaldon delivers one of the most heartbreaking farewells in romantic literature.

Narrator Davina Porter delivers another peerless performance, effortlessly handling aristocratic French court dialects and the raw grief of the Highland clans. Porter voices Claire’s emotional maturity and Jamie’s passionate resilience with soul-stirring authenticity. Alongside epics like The Pillars of the Earth and The Shadow of the Wind, this forty-three-hour recording represents the peak of Historical Fiction on a single credit.

Narrator Soundcheck: Davina Porter

Davina Porter’s narration on Dragonfly in Amber is a breathtaking exhibition of classical voice acting. Porter navigates courtly French phrasing, Latin medical terminology, and thick Highland brogues with effortless authority, never losing dramatic clarity over forty-three marathon hours. Her portrayal of Jamie’s agonizing emotional vulnerability at Craigh na Dun is devastatingly moving, while her timing during the Parisian espionage scenes maintains crackling suspense. Pristinely engineered with rich acoustic depth, this audiobook is an absolute masterwork.
