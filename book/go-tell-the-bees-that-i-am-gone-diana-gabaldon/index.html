About this Audiobook

In 1779–1780, Jamie and Claire are finally reunited with Brianna and Roger on Fraser's Ridge as the Revolutionary War sweeps into the southern backcountry.

The Southern Backcountry Campaign, Mountain Beekeeping, and the Fraser Family Reunited

*Go Tell the Bees That I Am Gone* is Diana Gabaldon’s ninth *Outlander* volume, spanning forty-nine and a half hours. The novel opens in 1779 with an unforgettable moment of emotional joy: Brianna, Roger, and their children have safely returned from the twentieth century, walking up the trail to reunite with Claire and Jamie Fraser at Fraser’s Ridge. Yet this long-awaited reunion takes place under the dark clouds of the American Revolutionary War, which has shifted into the brutal Southern backcountry of the Carolinas.

While Jamie and Claire fortify their settlement—tending honeybees through folklore rituals to ward off misfortune—tensions explode between Loyalist militias and Patriot partisans. Jamie takes up arms at Kings Mountain while Lord John Grey faces danger in Savannah. Gabaldon masterfully balances intimate mountain domesticity against the violent birth of a new nation and the mystery of time travel itself.

Narrator Davina Porter returns for what may be her final full-length performance in the series, delivering a warm, resonant masterclass. Porter’s portrayal of the aging Jamie and Claire radiates forty years of hard-won wisdom, marital devotion, and quiet strength, making every conversation on the Ridge feel sacred. For listeners seeking the ultimate immersion in Historical Fiction and family drama on a single credit, *Go Tell the Bees That I Am Gone* is an absolute triumph.

Porter brings an almost maternal tenderness to Claire’s reflections on aging and family, while maintaining Jamie’s unmistakable Scottish authority and courage.
