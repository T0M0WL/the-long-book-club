About this Audiobook

Oxford historian Kivrin Engle is sent back to the 14th century for field research, only to arrive in 1348 just as the Black Death ravages the English countryside.

Connie Willis's 26-Hour Monumental Hugo and Nebula-Winning Epic of Medieval Survival and Human Grace

Clocking in at over twenty-six monumental hours, *Doomsday Book* by Connie Willis is the undisputed crown jewel of historical time-travel literature, having swept both the Hugo and Nebula Awards upon publication. Set in mid-21st-century Oxford, where temporal field drops are managed by the university's history department under strict safety protocols, ambitious student Kivrin Engle prepares for an unprecedented research mission to the year 1320. But as Kivrin makes the jump, an unknown pandemic suddenly strikes modern Oxford, incapacitating the laboratory staff and causing an undetectable temporal slippage. Instead of landing safely in the 14th century's peaceful second decade, Kivrin awakens in 1348—the exact catastrophic arrival date of the Black Death in the English countryside.

In audio format, Jenny Sterlin delivers an extraordinary, emotionally staggering performance that handles the novel's dual-timeline structure with monumental grace. Sterlin masterfully captures the escalating contrast between modern Oxford's frantic, quarantine-choked bureaucratic panic led by the heroic Professor Dunworthy, and Kivrin’s quiet, heartbreaking immersion into a medieval manor whose villagers gradually fall victim to the pestilence. Listening to Kivrin provide palliative care, compassion, and human dignity to frightened villagers as their world crumbles is an acoustic experience of profound emotional power. Paralleling the rich historical texture of Diana Gabaldon’s Outlander and the devastating resilience found in The Stand, this twenty-six-hour epic elevates speculative time travel into high literature.

Investing a single monthly credit in twenty-six hours of *Doomsday Book* is one of the most rewarding listening commitments you can make. Willis eschews cheap Hollywood paradoxes to deliver a deeply researched, gut-wrenching study of ordinary human courage in the face of insurmountable catastrophe. Anchored by Jenny Sterlin’s timeless, compassionate narration, this landmark masterpiece transforms your headphone listening into an unforgettable historical pilgrimage.

Narrator Soundcheck: Jenny Sterlin

Narrator Jenny Sterlin delivers a majestic, emotionally resonant performance that honors Connie Willis's historical masterpiece across twenty-six hours of uninterrupted listening. Sterlin transitions effortlessly between the frantic, upper-crust academic panic of modern Oxford and the gentle, archaic cadences of medieval Oxfordshire villagers. Her portrayal of Kivrin Engle balances youthful academic ambition with boundless, tear-inducing empathy during the novel’s devastating third-act plague sequences. With steady, unhurried pacing, clear diction, and immaculate acoustic clarity, Sterlin’s narration makes this harrowing historical marathon feel deeply intimate and profoundly human.
